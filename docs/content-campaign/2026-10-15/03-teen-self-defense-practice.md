# Teen Self-Defense in Greene County: What We Practice

When families ask me about teen self-defense classes in Greene County, I start with what we teach.

I want teens to feel sure of themselves. We work on control of space and ways to talk with adults. Help from an adult comes first.

BJJ also gives teens a place to practice calm choices through movement with a partner.

At our studio in Tannersville, I connect those ideas to clear tasks on the mat.

## Start with space and help

Teens need ways to think about the space around them. They also need to know whom they can ask for help.

I teach calm use of BJJ when adult help is out of reach.

We talk about how to choose what to do. In class, teens can try a task and think about their choices.

The aim is learning. Each student brings their own needs, questions, and pace.

## Learn to connect and create space

In some tasks, teens work to stay in contact with a partner. In others, they find a way to break that contact.

We work on ways to move with care. We also work on pins. Teens learn how those holds fit partners of more than one size or skill level.

A partner's size, skill, and moves all change the task. Teens try to make sense of those changes as they train.

I give simple goals and feedback as they work.

## Practice with more than one partner

Students work in pairs and groups of three. They also take part in tasks with the larger class.

They can try an idea, hear feedback, and see how it fits the next round of practice. Their questions matter to me.

If my words seem unclear, I want to hear that.

## What habits matter in class?

Clean bodies and clean training gear are part of caring for a partner.

Teens also learn to move with care. They learn to follow the task, ask for help, and respond to taps.

Before tasks that involve holds ending in a tap, students practice tapping.

We share these rules for our time on the mat. Parents can ask me how we teach them.

## Will group class fit my teen?

Group class gives teens shared tasks and time with training partners. In [private coaching](/private-lessons), we can spend more time on one student's goal. Ask me what may fit your teen.

Tell me what your teen hopes to learn. We can discuss the group and a useful next step.

What Happens in Your First BJJ Class, Minute by Minute

Your first class follows a clear path. You meet Sandy, learn the room and safety rules, practice simple movements, and begin skill-based resistance activities with a carefully matched partner.

Arrive 5–10 minutes early · Wear clean athletic clothes · Pause anytime · Beginner-guided from the first minute

Before Class: Map Your First Step

Training begins with a free 15-minute BJJ Goal Mapping Session.

You and Sandy discuss:

What you want from training
Your current activity level
Past injuries or movement concerns
The days you can attend consistently
The class lane that fits you best

You leave knowing which class to attend, what to wear, and how your first visit will work.

What to wear

Wear a clean, fitted athletic shirt with shorts, leggings, or athletic pants. Choose clothing without zippers, buttons, or loose pockets.

Bring a water bottle. Leave watches, jewelry, and street shoes off the mat.

Your First 60 Minutes
Minute 0–5
Arrive and Settle In

Walk upstairs to the second-floor studio at 6045 Main Street. Sandy greets you and shows you where to place your shoes, coat, water, and personal items.

You will see the training area before class starts. This gives you time to take in the room and ask a question without feeling rushed.

Minute 5–10
Learn the Safety Signals

Sandy explains the most important safety tool in Jiu-Jitsu: the tap.

You can tap your partner, tap the mat, or say “tap.” The activity stops immediately. You can also say “pause” whenever you need an explanation, water, or a moment to reset.

You will learn how partners communicate, how each activity restarts, and how the coach controls the pace.

Minute 10–20
Prepare the Movements

The first movement activities prepare the same shapes and actions you will use later in class.

You may practice:

Building a stable base
Moving your hips
Turning safely
Keeping balanced posture
Making comfortable contact with a partner

Every movement can be adjusted around your knees, back, neck, flexibility, or current fitness level.

Minute 20–35
Learn Your First Position

Sandy introduces one useful position and gives each partner a simple job.

You might learn how to:

Maintain a stable position
Create enough space to move
Protect your balance
Escape from underneath
Follow a moving partner safely

The explanation stays brief. You begin moving, notice what happens, and receive coaching while you practice.

Minute 35–50
Begin Skill-Based Resistance

Your partner now gives you a realistic problem to solve at a controlled pace.

Each activity includes:

A clear starting position
One job for each partner
A small set of allowed actions
A clear success condition
A reset whenever someone succeeds or taps

Sandy chooses your partner, watches the activity, and adjusts the resistance. The challenge grows only when your movement and decisions are ready for it.

What “resistance” means here

Resistance gives your skill a real problem to solve.

It does not require an open round with every technique available. Your first activities stay focused on one position, one problem, and a few useful choices. This lets you experience live Jiu-Jitsu while keeping the task understandable.

Minute 50–57
Reset, Repeat, and Improve

You return to the same task with one small coaching point.

The second attempt usually feels clearer because you now recognize the starting position, the goal, and the moment when you need to act.

Progress in the first class may look simple:

You kept your balance longer
You remembered to breathe
You created space
You noticed the escape sooner
You tapped calmly and restarted

Each result gives Sandy information for your next class.

Minute 57–60
Close the Class and Map the Next Visit

Class ends with a short reset and a chance to ask questions.

Sandy helps you choose the next class that fits your schedule. You leave knowing what you practiced, what comes next, and how to build a training rhythm you can maintain.

You do not need to master Jiu-Jitsu during your first hour. Your first job is to understand the room, experience the training process, and return for the next lesson.

Frequently Asked Questions
Do I need a gi for my first class?

You can begin in clean athletic clothes. Sandy will tell you whether your selected class uses a gi or no-gi clothing.

Will I have to wrestle an experienced student?

Sandy matches your partner and controls the task. Your first activities use specific positions, limited goals, and a pace selected for you.

What happens if I get tired?

Pause, breathe, and get water. The activities can be shortened or adjusted while you learn.

Do I need to get in shape first?

Your starting fitness level helps Sandy choose the correct pace. Training develops your movement, conditioning, and confidence over time.

## 1. Prepare your clips

Keep the original videos on your phone and a second copy where practical. Avoid moving or deleting source files while the draft still uses them. Make room for the exported video.

Choose the clearest complete talking take for each S01–S11 block. Favor clear speech, steady framing, and natural delivery. A take with a small pause is easy to trim. A clipped word or distorted voice is harder to repair.

Keep class footage, room footage, the social ending, and the separate discovery script available, but leave them out of the first import. Use student footage only where permission covers this use. Check background mirrors and screens too.

**Checkpoint:** You can identify eleven talking blocks and the alternate ending.

Use **Canvas** to set **9:16** if you filmed upright for vertical delivery. If you filmed sideways, choose the intended output ratio after checking the crop. Keep your face and important movement visible; a tight vertical crop can lose both partners in a wide class shot.

## 3. Trim speech before adding pictures

Select S01 and use its trim handles or **Trim/Precut** controls to remove the time before you start speaking and after you finish. Repeat for the other ten clips. Keep complete first and last sounds and natural breaths.

For a mistake inside a take, put the playhead before the mistake, choose **Split**, move to the end of the mistake, split again, select only that middle section, and delete it. Replay the join. If the words jump or a breath sounds chopped, undo and leave more space, or use a cleaner take.

Use timeline zoom where available for fine cuts. Replay a few seconds before and after every join. Speech should sound like a person talking, with enough pause to understand the next thought.

Keep these facts intact: adults start with a visit; Sandy confirms training; kids and teens have their own plan with a parent or guardian; the first class is free; continued training has paid plans.

Watch the full spoken sequence before continuing. Note its actual length. **Do not pad the film or speed up your voice to reach four minutes.** The 240-second figure in older planning files was an edit allocation, not a required running time.

**Checkpoint:** Every sentence is understandable, every join sounds clean, and the offer still makes sense.

## 4. Cover matching speech with class and room footage

The talking clips remain your main timeline. Add a cutaway as **PIP** above them. This lets the viewer see the class while hearing your original speech.

For each cutaway:

1. Put the playhead at the sentence it illustrates.
2. Choose **PIP**, then select the matching video.
3. Select that PIP layer and enlarge/reposition it to cover the canvas where appropriate. Review the crop before confirming.
4. Set the **PIP clip's Volume to 0**. Leave the main talking clip audible.
5. Trim the PIP layer and adjust its start/end to match the spoken idea.
6. Play through both edges. Your speech should continue smoothly as the picture changes.

Start with short cutaways of roughly 2–5 seconds, but let the action decide the length. A connected demonstration may need longer. Return to your face when introducing a new idea or explaining a choice. Use the spoken cues below rather than fixed timestamps, since actual take lengths are unknown.

| --- | --- |
| S01 | Start on your face. An adult arrival can cover “Come learn a new skill” if it fits. |
| S02 | Show your coaching at “I coach you step by step.” |
| S03 | Match “I show a move” to the cue; “You try it” to the learner attempt; “a clear tip” to feedback; the last “You try the move” to the retry. Use a connected sequence if you have it. Keep your face if that proof is missing. |
| S04 | Keep your face for your introduction. Add a permission-cleared lineage image only if it matches the background you describe. Show real coaching for the goals, room, and partner lines. |
| S05 | Show the room at “I show you the room,” and matched adult practice at “with a matched partner at your pace.” Keep enough movement visible to understand the action. |

| S10 | Optional current booking-screen recording with personal information excluded. If the recording is outdated, use your face. |

Avoid stretching footage, looping a learner's attempt as if it were progress, or covering a missing claim with unrelated smiles. Plain picture changes are enough. If a shot does not fit, omit it.

**Checkpoint:** Each cutaway explains the sentence underneath it; voice remains continuous; class audio is muted.

## 6. Add and correct captions

If your version includes **Auto Captions**, generate them from the main speech after the cuts are finished. If that feature is unavailable, add timed text manually in short phrases. A paid feature is optional for this workflow.

Listen and correct each caption against the actual recording. Pay special attention to Sandy, Clockwork Jiu Jitsu, Brooklyn, Tannersville, Catskills, Beginner Lane, and START. Captions should represent what you actually say. If a take omits an essential offer fact, replace or rerecord that line; captions alone cannot repair the speech.

Use one or two short lines per caption. Align each phrase with the voice. Place captions in a consistent lower-middle region above likely interface controls, and move block titles higher if they collide. Check legibility over both your face and class footage.

**Checkpoint:** Watch on mute. You can follow the whole message, including confirmation and paid continuation.

## 7. Balance sound and finish the picture

Play adjacent talking clips and adjust their individual **Volume** controls until your voice feels consistent. Avoid pushing a distorted recording louder. Check with headphones for joins and doubled audio, then with the phone speaker for everyday clarity.

Finish a speech-only version first. Music is optional. If adding a track, use music you have rights to use at the intended destination. Bring it down until every word is easy to understand, especially in S06 and S09. Apply gentle fades if the controls are available. Slider percentages are relative controls; they do not measure loudness or decibels.

Use small brightness/color adjustments only where shots visibly clash. Keep skin natural. Review a moving section after any adjustment, not just a paused frame. Plain cuts are the default; avoid effects that interrupt the message.

**Checkpoint:** Voice is clear on the phone speaker, and changing clips causes no distracting jump in sound or picture.

**Checkpoint:** Each exported version gives one clear action appropriate to where it will appear.

## 9. Review before export

Watch once from beginning to end with sound, without scrubbing. Then watch on mute. Check complete words, correct sequence, matching cutaways, clean joins, accurate captions, readable titles, the full first-class explanation, and the right ending. Check the first frame and last frame for blank gaps.

Confirm that people shown have suitable permissions and that booking screens contain no private information. Remove accidental prices, spot counters, old schedules, or unrelated graphics.

**Checkpoint:** You would be comfortable sending this exact video to someone asking how their first visit works.

## 10. Export and inspect the saved file

Tap the export/save control. Choose **1080p** for a standard full-HD output when appropriate to the source. Match the source frame rate where possible: usually 30 fps for footage recorded at 30 fps, or 60 fps if recorded at 60 fps and that is useful for the delivery. Avoid converting to a higher frame rate just because it is offered. A larger export cannot restore detail absent from the original. With mixed sources, prioritize the main talking footage and inspect motion in the result.

Keep the draft and source videos. Use descriptive file names where your Android file manager allows it:

- `SSBJJ-VSL-Web-2026-10-08.mp4`
- `SSBJJ-VSL-Social-2026-10-08.mp4`
- `SSBJJ-Discovery-2026-10-08.mp4`

Open the exported file in your phone's video player. Play it through to the end with sound. Check the final CTA, caption timing, crop, picture quality, and any watermark or export issue. Sample on mute too. Export success alone does not prove the file plays correctly.

**Done:** The saved file plays correctly from first frame to final CTA, and your draft/originals remain available. Posting and website placement are separate actions.

## Fixes when something looks wrong

| Problem | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Class footage appears as a little box | Select the PIP layer and enlarge/reposition it. Check the crop of both partners. If a full-frame crop loses the action, choose a different shot or intentional framing. |
| Two voices or room noise play at once | Mute the selected PIP clip; preserve main talking audio. Check any added audio track too. |
| Speech disappears under a cutaway | Check that the talking clip remains in the main timeline and its volume is active. Undo an accidental replacement/deletion. |
| A cut clips a word | Extend that clip edge or undo and trim again with more space. Replay the join. |
| Captions drift | Fix their timing after the final speech cuts. Later main-timeline cuts can require caption and PIP timing repairs. |
| Title and captions overlap | Move the title higher or shorten its on-screen duration; retain the approved wording. |
| Black bars or excessive crop | Review Canvas and the selected layer's fit. Choose framing that preserves the action; avoid stretching people. |
| A needed shot is missing | Keep Sandy on camera. Add a matching shot later if useful. |
| Export fails or the phone runs out of space | Preserve the draft, check free storage, close other apps, and retry at the intended resolution. Keep source files the project still uses. |
| You get lost in polish | Return to this order: understandable speech, matching pictures, readable text, clear sound, correct CTA, verified export. |

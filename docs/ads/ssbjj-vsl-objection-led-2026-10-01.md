## Structure and application

The transcript's opening uses promise, pain, proof and plan, followed by an explanation of the method, objections, and a CTA. Its objection pattern is belief → correction → better approach → proof. Apply that logic in the planning notes; keep the spoken copy positive and respectful. Avoid declaring a viewer's concern “wrong.” Show how Sandy helps them take the next step.

## Sandy's spoken master

### 1. Promise and beginner concern

“You can learn Jiu-Jitsu one step at a time.

I'm Sandy. I teach kids, teens, and adults here in Tannersville, on the mountaintop.

You may want to learn for yourself. You may want to find a class for your child. You might be asking what your first day will be like.

I'll show you how we start, how I coach, and how we plan your first class.”

### 2. Proof and starting plan

“I coach students as they practice. I give them a task, watch them try, and help them with the next step.

You can start with a free visit. We spend 15 minutes on your goals. Wear your day clothes. I'll show you the mats and walk you through safety.

We talk about what you want to learn and the days that fit your week. I help you choose and schedule your first class.

In that class, you learn how to move with a partner. We use games and practice to build your skills. I coach you at a pace that fits you.”

### 3. Getting ready to start

“You might be thinking about your fitness. Tell me what feels good and what you want help with. We can talk about your starting pace at the visit.

In class, I help you work through the task in front of you. You get to try, ask a question, and practice the next step.”

### 4. Learning from the start

“You might be wondering what to do on the mat. I show you the task and help you learn it.

You can ask me to show a step again. We work through it as you practice with a partner. You have a coach to help you start.”

### 5. Partner pace and safety

“You might want to see how partner practice works. At the visit, we walk through safety and talk about your questions.

In class, I help you learn the task and work at your pace. You can see how I coach students through practice.”

### 6. Making room in your week

“You might be working out how a class fits your week. Bring those questions to the visit.

We can look at the class schedule and talk about the days that work for you. I help you plan your first class so you know your next step.”

### 7. Finding a start for your child

“If your child wants to learn, we can talk about their goals as well as yours. I teach kids and teens, and I help new adults start.

We can talk about the right class for each of you. You can see how I coach young students and how I coach adults.”

### 8. Next step

“Come meet me and see the room. Your first visit is free, and your first class is free.

Tap the link to reserve your free first visit. We'll talk about your goals and plan your start.

Start calm. Train smart.”

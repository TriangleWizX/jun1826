CDC Physical Activity & Health Benchmarks

This reference page details the public health standards established by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) for adult physical health, cardiovascular fitness, and resistance training, and how Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu aligns with these recommendations.

Summary of CDC Guidelines for Adults

The CDC recommends that adults engage in at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic physical activity per week (or 75 minutes of vigorous-intensity activity), paired with muscle-strengthening activities on 2 or more days per week that work all major muscle groups (legs, hips, back, abdomen, chest, shoulders, and arms).

Meeting these guidelines is proven to reduce the risk of cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, and certain cancers, while improving mood, mental health, cognitive clarity, and sleep quality.

How Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu Fulfills Public Health Guidance

Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu is unique because it integrates both aerobic cardiovascular exercise and multi-planar bodyweight resistance into every class session:

  • Full-Body Functional Strength: Dynamic isometric holds, guard passes, and bodyweight posture control engage core, back, and hip muscles naturally without joint-straining heavy barbell loads.
  • Cardiovascular Conditioning: Continuous movement during positional drills elevates heart rate into optimal aerobic and anaerobic training zones.
  • Neuro-Muscular Coordination & Balance: Learning weight distribution, breakfalls, and spatial awareness enhances proprioception and balance, reducing fall risks as adults age.
  • Mental Stress Relief: The tactical, problem-solving nature of BJJ requires full present-moment focus, lowering cortisol and building mental resilience.

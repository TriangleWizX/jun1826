Jiu jitsu terms for your first class

By Sandy Jose Nunez · Updated October 24, 2026

Reserve Free Intro

Brazilian Jiu Jitsu has special words. You only need a few jiu jitsu terms. You use them on your first visit. Learn five simple words before class. You learn the rest on the mat with your coach. Every drill moves at a calm pace.

The safety word comes first: the tap

Safety guides every move in our school. One word protects each student on the mat.

A tap is the safety signal that tells your training partner to reset. Tap the mat with your hand. You can tap your partner. You can also say the word aloud. Your partner lets go right away. Both students reset. Both students learn with care.

Words for safety and culture

Our room stays friendly and calm. These terms guide how students train together.

  • Tap. A tap is the safety signal that tells your training partner to reset.
  • Safety. Safety in BJJ means clear taps, calm moves, partner care, and coach help.
  • Beginner Lane. The Beginner Lane is where new students learn. You learn rules, partner care, and paced movement.

What you wear and where you train

You train in clean gym gear. Here are the simple words you hear.

  • Gi. A gi is a clean cotton uniform. It has a jacket and pants made for fabric grips.
  • No-Gi. No-gi is training in shorts and a rash guard. You use wrist grips and body control.
  • Mat. The mat is the soft, clean floor. Students practice moves with care on the mat.

Body balance and making space

BJJ uses leverage instead of brute force. These moves help you move with ease.

  • Base. Base is a steady body hold. It helps you keep your balance.
  • Frame. A frame is a body structure. It helps you create space and block pressure.
  • Bridge. A bridge is lifting your hips off the mat. It creates space and relieves pressure.
  • Shrimp. Shrimp is a hip escape move. It creates space and improves your spot.

Core mat positions

Ground grappling uses clear resting spots. You learn how each spot works step by step.

  • Guard. Guard is a bottom position. You use your legs, hips, and frames to control the top partner.
  • Mount. Mount is a strong top position. One person sits over the other person’s torso.
  • Side Control. Side control is a top hold. You hold across your partner’s upper body.

Class action and practice words

During class, your coach shares clear cues. These words guide your partner practice.

  • Drilling. Drilling is practicing a move step by step. You work with a helpful partner to build clean habits.
  • Sweep. A sweep is a coached move. It takes you from the bottom position safely to the top.
  • Escape. An escape is a coached movement. It helps you move from underneath back to safety.

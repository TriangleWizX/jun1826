Agency-reported case summary

Marietta Police Department’s Reported 2020 BJJ Outcomes

Research Snapshot

Compared with coworkers who chose not to take part, Marietta Police Department reported these 2020 results for officers in its Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu program:

  • 48% fewer injuries to officers who used force
  • 53% fewer injuries to people arrested when force was used
  • 23% less Taser use

The police shared these results. Officers chose to join the program. We cannot tell if BJJ caused the change. Other teams may get different results. The report leaves out group sizes and tests of chance.

Program Characteristics

In its February 8, 2021 article, the department described these parts of its program:

  • Instructor: The department named outside instructor Humberto Borges.
  • Paid training time: The department said it paid officers for class time and covered class costs.
  • New hires: New officers had to attend at least one session each week through academy and field training.
  • Other officers: All officers could sign up for as many as three classes each week.
  • Comparison: The article reports 2020 figures for officers who took part and coworkers who chose not to take part.

Important Limitations

The public article gives a short comparison, not a full study report:

Who took part: Officers chose whether to join. The article does not describe random assignment.

Some facts are missing from the report. How many staff took part? How often did each group use force? How many cases were there? How did the groups differ at the start? Was the gap due to chance? The report leaves these questions open.

Details not reported: The article gives no participant count, incident counts, statistical tests, exposure rates, or method for accounting for differences between groups.

What it can show: It records what one department reported. It cannot show that BJJ caused the differences or predict results for another agency.

Application to Sensei Sandy BJJ

This report is about one police team. It did not test our classes. It cannot tell us what our classes would change.

Any Sensei Sandy agency pilot requires a separate discussion of department policy, training objectives, participant safety, equipment, supervision, and New York state requirements.

For athletes and parents

What Jiu-Jitsu Cross-Training Can Offer

Jiu-Jitsu is a coached class you can add alongside a main sport. The coach guides the class and sets the pace. It teaches Jiu-Jitsu skills; it does not promise better results in another sport.

What students practice

  • Balance while moving with a partner
  • How to fall and stand with coach guidance
  • How to make choices during partner practice

What this class can and cannot tell you

Students practice Jiu-Jitsu skills and movement with a coach. This class does not promise better skiing, soccer, running, or other sport results. Class fit should take the athlete’s age, season, schedule, and needs into account. Ask a qualified health professional about medical concerns.

Research and limits

The American Academy of Pediatrics discusses sport sampling and cautions against early intensive specialization. Long-term athlete-development research supports a nuanced picture: adult world-class athletes often had broader childhood sport experience, while junior results can follow different patterns. These sources do not promise an immediate competitive benefit from BJJ.

Research describes BJJ as intermittent, full-body work involving dynamic and isometric strength and endurance demands. Adults still need an appropriate overall mix of aerobic and muscle-strengthening activity.

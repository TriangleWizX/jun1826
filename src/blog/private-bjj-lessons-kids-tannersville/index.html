What to Look for in a Coach

A youth coach needs good skills and patience. When you look for a coach in the Catskills, check these facts:

  • Real Black Belt Rank: Ask who gave the coach their belt. Coach Sandy Jose Nunez earned his black belt from Josh Griffiths at Clockwork Jiu-Jitsu in New York City. Ask how the coach teaches and checks each skill.
  • Teaching Kids: Teaching kids takes patience and care. A good coach makes hard moves simple and fun.
  • Safety First: The coach guides every drill. They keep the room calm and friendly.
  • Open Talks: A good coach answers your questions. They share each step of your childs path.

Teaching Kids and Teens

Children grow fast. A good coach shapes each class for the students age.

Kids Ages Five to Nine: Fun Moves and Balance

Young kids learn best through fun games. Coaches teach:

  • Animal games like bear crawls to build strength.
  • Safe falls to land softly on the mat.
  • Respect for personal space and using a clear voice.
  • Balance drills with a gentle partner.
  • Kind praise that rewards good focus.

Teens Ages Ten to Seventeen: Smart Moves and Poise

Teens face tests at school. Private lessons help teens build calm focus:

  • Body leverage helps smaller students handle force.
  • Calm escapes from holds and standing tall.
  • Setting clear boundaries with peers.
  • Cool heads under gentle pressure.
  • Good routines that build quiet pride.

A Sample Lesson Plan

This is a sample lesson flow. Ask Sandy which steps fit your child. Ask about parent viewing before you book:

  • Warm-Up: Light stretches and gentle rolls warm up the body.
  • Skill Time: The coach teaches one new move. The coach shows each step at a slow pace. The student tries the move with direct help.
  • Partner Drills: The student practices with the coach. Skill-based resistance activities begin at the right pace from day one. The coach guides every move.
  • Review: The coach recaps the lesson. The coach praises good work. The student gets a clear goal for next time.

Safety on the Mat

Safety comes first for kids. Good gyms keep high standards:

  • Clean Mats: Ask the coach about mat care. Tour the room before class.
  • Coach Care: Ask how the coach guides drills and checks the pace.
  • Tap Early: Students learn to tap early. Tapping is a safe signal that builds trust.
  • Open Viewing: Ask about parent viewing before you book.
  • Calm Pacing: Clear drills keep the room peaceful.

Five Questions to Ask a Coach

Ask these five questions at a gym:

  • 1. Where did you earn your black belt? Make sure the coach trained with a real team.
  • 2. How do you coach shy or active kids? A good coach matches your childs pace.
  • 3. Can parents watch each class? Good gyms welcome parents to watch.
  • 4. How do you teach clear boundaries? Great classes teach firm words and calm posture.
  • 5. How do private lessons lead to group classes? Ask how private training helps kids join group mats.

Warning Signs to Watch For

Parents should watch for these signs at other gyms:

  • Pushy sales talks that demand long contracts before a class.
  • Surprise fees for tests or extra gear.
  • Dirty mats or messy floors.
  • Rough drills where students train unguided.
  • Rules that keep parents from watching.

How Private and Group Classes Work Together

Private lessons and group classes help each other:

  • A Gentle Start: One-on-one time builds core skills and calm confidence.
  • Targeted Help: If your child gets stuck on a move, ask the coach for help with that skill.
  • Smooth Shift to Groups: Kids who know the basics step into group classes with ease.
  • Team Fun: Group classes bring friends and diverse partners. Private sessions sharpen personal skill.

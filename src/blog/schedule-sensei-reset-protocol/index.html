What the sources say

  • WHO says some movement is better than none. More can help. Its guidance covers activity across your life. WHO
  • One habit study found a midpoint of 66 days. This was the time for habits to feel more natural. People varied. It is a study result, not a fixed clock. Lally et al., Wiley
  • A review found a wide range: 4 to 335 days. The time to form a habit varies across people and tasks. PMC
  • An if-then plan links a cue to an action. For example: when work ends, pack the class bag. Gollwitzer & Brandstatter, 1997
  • The cited paper links if-then plans with starting a task. It is general planning research, rather than a BJJ class test. Cancer Control

Choose your reset level

Level 1: You missed 1 week

  • Go to the next small-group class.
  • Keep your usual class days if they fit.
  • Keep the restart at your usual pace.

Level 2: You missed 2 to 4 weeks

  • If needed, do one class this week.
  • Ask about adding a second class next week.
  • Aim to show up and learn one step.

Level 3: You missed 2+ months

  • Start with one class this week.
  • Use two days as an example for four weeks.
  • Ask about more class time when you feel ready.

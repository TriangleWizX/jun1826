Seated Guard Basics

In seated guard, you sit and face your partner. Use your hands and feet to manage space. Ask your coach to check your base.

Reaching too far can pull you off balance. Keep your elbows close and head steady. Your coach can help you reset. Reserve Free Intro.

Work through posture, frames, space, and entries. Use this guide with a coach on mats.

The Seated Guard Safety Checklist

  • Seated forward, but your head stays behind your shoulders.
  • Knees, elbows, and shoulders stack in the same vertical zone.
  • Hands ready to connect to your partner or the floor.
  • Feet ready to manage distance.
  • If you lose inside position, reset to supine.

1) Seated Guard Posture and Base

Keep your hips forward and head over your base. Hold your elbows close to your body. Keep your hands and feet ready to move.

If you lean too far, your partner can pull you down or circle past you. Stay close to your base.

Be ready to grip, post a hand, or sit back.

2) Hand Fighting and Safe Frames

Work for inside hand position. Clear grips that pull you out of posture.

If your partner pushes past your shoulders, lie back into guard. Ask your coach to check the reset.

What safe framing feels like

  • Elbows close to ribs.
  • Knees close to elbows.
  • Frames are short.
  • Keep your hands ready to post for support.

Long reaches can expose your arms and neck. Keep your frames close.

3) Space and Balance

Keep your partner in front of you. Move between your hips with good posture. Ask your coach to check your range.

The knees-down emergency reset

If your partner circles, turn to face them. Use inside hand position and a knees-down reset. Practice this turn with your coach.

4) Simple Entries: Leg-Grab Trap to Single Leg or Collar Tie

If your partner grabs your near leg, you can work on a grip trap. Ask your coach to show the sequence.

  1. Step that foot slightly to the outside.
  2. Bring your second foot in to guide their grip and keep your base.
  3. Overgrip their grip and force their hand toward the mat.
  4. With your free hand, pull their same-side shoulder toward you.

Watch how they post the free hand as you pull their shoulder. Use that opening to rise at your coach's pace.

Finish option A: come up to a single leg

Bring both knees down and move forward. Cross their center line to collect a single leg. Keep your head and base steady.

Finish option B: collar tie to slow the scramble

If the leg entry is blocked, ask about a collar tie. Guide the head with care as you face your partner.

Keep your base, trap the grip, and rise with control.

5) Two Ways to Reach Top Position

Practice these two options with a coach. Start at a slow pace on mats.

Option 1: Move to Their Back

Start with the grip trap. If they stay planted, move to their side and take the back. Ask your coach to check the angle.

Option 2: Move to Chest-to-Chest Control

If they sit back, use your knees as a base. Move forward to chest-to-chest control as they lose balance. Your coach can help you time this move.

Common Problems and Quick Fixes

  • Getting shoved flat: elbows in, knees close, accept the supine reset.
  • They run around your head: knees-down reset and face them again.
  • Getting snapped down on the rise: trap a grip, pull a shoulder, then rise.

A 10-Minute Drill Plan

  • Posture reps (2 minutes): seated stack, switch hips, return to stack.
  • Inside hands (2 minutes): pummel for inside position maintaining connected elbows.
  • Knees-down reset (2 minutes): partner circles, you drop knees, face them.
  • Leg-grab trap (4 minutes): partner grabs leg, pin grip and pull shoulder, come up.

Ask your coach which drill fits your level. Use it to practice one clear skill.

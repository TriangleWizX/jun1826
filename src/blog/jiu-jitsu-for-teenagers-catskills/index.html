Teenage Jiu Jitsu: A Guide to Starting Class

Teens can learn new skills one step at a time.

What is teenage jiu jitsu?

Jiu jitsu is a sport where people learn to move with a partner. Teens learn body control, balance, and ways to move on the mat. The coach shows each skill and sets the pace.

Teens practice one task at a time. They may learn how to fall with care, move from one spot to the next, or find a safe way to get free. A partner helps them try each move. The coach stays close and gives clear tips.

Each class is a chance to listen, try, and learn. Teens can ask for help. They learn how to work with a partner and follow class rules.

Can a new teen start?

Yes. New students can start with basic moves and learn as they go. The coach can show each step and help each teen find a partner.

A teen may feel shy at first. Meeting the coach and seeing the room can make the plan clear. Parents can share what their teen wants to learn and ask how class works.

What happens at the first visit?

First, your teen meets Sandy and sees the room. A parent or guardian stays on site. Sandy confirms which class fits and what to wear.

Then your teen can try a class with coach help. They learn the room rules and basic ways to move. The coach explains the task. A partner drill gives them time to try it.

Each teen learns at a pace that fits the class. They can take a short pause, ask a question, and start again when ready. The goal is to learn the next skill with care.

What skills do teens learn?

Teens learn how to move with balance and care. They practice body control, safe falls, and simple grappling skills. They learn to use clear words with a partner and follow the coach’s steps.

Jiu jitsu also gives teens a place to work on a skill over time. Each class has small steps. A teen can notice what feels easier and ask what to work on next.

These are skills to practice. Each teen grows at their own pace. Class is a place to learn with a coach and a team.

How can parents help?

Talk with your teen before the visit. Ask what they hope to learn. Tell the coach what helps your teen feel ready to try a new class.

  • Look at the class plan together.
  • Ask who will coach the class.
  • Ask how partners are picked.
  • Ask what your teen should wear.
  • Give your teen time to share how it felt.

A clear plan can help the first day feel calm. Your teen can meet the coach, see the space, and learn what comes next.

Questions teens often ask

Do I need to be fit first?

You can start with the skills you have today. Tell the coach what you need. The coach can help you take each step at a good pace.

What if I feel shy?

That is okay. Meet Sandy and see the room first. You can ask a question and learn what the class will do.

Can my parent watch?

Yes. A parent or guardian stays on site for the first visit. They can see the class and talk with Sandy.

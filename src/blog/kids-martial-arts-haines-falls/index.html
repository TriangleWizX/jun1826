Why Jiu-Jitsu Is Different From Some Other Kids Activities

Jiu-jitsu teaches how to move when a partner is close. Holds and pressure may feel new. The coach sets clear limits. Kids learn to breathe, tap, make space, and ask for help.

A drill shows what needs more work. A child may lose balance or miss a step. The coach can show that step again. Kids learn through safe tries and clear help.

Watch for small changes. Does your child listen to the coach? Do they care for their partner? Can they reset and try again? These signs may tell you more than who wins a game.

How to Judge Progress Over the First Month

Watch how your child joins class. Do they know the tap rule? Can they follow a step? Do they care for their partner? Can they try again? A stripe or game win is one part of learning.

Each child shows growth in their own way. One may slow down. One may speak up. Another may join a drill with more ease. Ask the coach what they see over time.

A hard class can happen. Ask how the coach helped your child. Did they get a clear next step? Could they rejoin at a pace that fit? Talk with Sandy about what to try next.

The fast answer: the 5 Sensei Rules

  1. Seek peace. Walk away, make space, and get an adult.
  2. If attacked, protect your head. Keep balance. Get safe and ask for help.
  3. Use calm words, a clear voice, and help from an adult. Holds are for protection when needed.
  4. Use holds and clear words. Keep strikes out of class.
  5. Apply holds with care. Move slowly. Release as soon as your partner taps.

Rules for bullying and conflict

Bullying has a clear meaning in the sources.

Bullying involves unwanted aggressive acts and a power gap. The gap may be real or felt. CDC also includes repeat acts or acts likely to recur. [2] [3]

In 2021 to 2022, about 19% of students ages 12 to 18 said they faced bullying at school. That figure comes from NCES. [4]

Kids need clear words and a way to get help. Practice both with a coach.

The 3 T Steps we teach

  • Talk: use a calm voice. Ask for space.
  • Tell: use a clear, firm voice. Ask them to step back.
  • Tackle means tie up for protection only when needed. Use control, get safe, and get help.

Use control and clear words

Strikes can raise the risk in a conflict. Class teaches holds with clear limits. The coach helps partners stay at a calm pace.

A study looked at harm in BJJ contests. Read the source for its limits. Its findings alone cannot rank all sports for safety. [5]

Contact sports carry risk. Tap rules and partner care help manage that risk. Ask about health concerns before class. Learn the class words

Use slow holds and release on tap

Give your partner time to tap. Release the hold at once when they tap.

A 2025 BJJ study counted harm in training and contests. It found 5.5 cases per 1,000 training hours. It found 55.9 per 1,000 contest matches. Those are different units. [6]

In a 2021 study, two out of three athletes reported harm in three years. The event kept them from training for at least two weeks. Read the study for its scope. [7]

Release on tap. Every time.

Respect your class and partner

  • Listen to coach the first time.
  • Be kind to each person.
  • Line up at the start and end of class.
  • Shake hands at the end of class.
  • Raise your hand for questions.
  • Use control and take care of your partner.
  • Tap early. Release as soon as your partner taps.
  • Pause at once when a partner asks or the coach calls time.
  • Use kind words. Tell the coach about bullying.

Adults should act when bullying happens. Use the source guide to learn how to help. [8] Learn the class words

Keep gear and the room clean

Clean mats and clothes are part of partner care.

1) Wash gear after each use

CDC advises athletes to wash uniforms, towels, and clothes after each use. [9]

2) Leave shoes off the mat

Shoes can bring germs to the mat. Some germs live on surfaces for hours, days, or weeks. [10]

3) Clean shared gear

CDC advises cleaning shared gear after each use. Let it dry before the next use. [11]

4) Tell the coach about skin concerns

Skin germs can spread in contact sports. CDC lists ringworm, a herpes skin illness, and germs such as MRSA among wrestlers. Tell the coach about a rash. Ask a health professional when it is safe to train. [12]

Tell the coach if you feel dizzy or need a break. Pause and get help when a task feels wrong.

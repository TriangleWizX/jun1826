Upright Posture Speaks First

A person communicates before speaking a single word. When stress arrives, teens often round their shoulders and look down.

Coach Sandy guides teens into an upright, balanced stance. Students practice holding the head high with level eyes. Shoulders rest back with relaxed arms. Feet plant firm on the floor at shoulder width.

Students project calm self-respect across school corridors with this grounded stance. Classmates perceive poise and peaceful intent. The student feels centered from within.

Setting Clear Vocal Boundaries

Clear words carry great authority. Coach Sandy teaches students how to combine clear speech with assertive body language.

In structured partner drills, teens practice looking a partner straight in the eye. They bring two open hands up to chest level with relaxed palms. They speak direct, respectful words: "Please step back" or "Give me my space."

Students create a natural barrier with open palms while signaling peaceful intentions. Teens practice these verbal cues until speaking up feels natural. Students also practice seeking help from teachers, coaches, and family members.

Safe Physical Control Through Leverage

Physical contact sometimes occurs in tight school spaces. Coach Sandy teaches teens to manage distance and balance through safe grappling holds.

Students practice controlling grips at the wrists and elbows. If another student advances, the teen steps into a stable base and establishes an arm frame. The teen keeps distance and neutralizes forward momentum through pure mechanical leverage.

This method maintains safety for everyone. The teen controls the situation with patience and composure while awaiting adult assistance.

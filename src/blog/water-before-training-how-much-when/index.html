How Much Water to Drink Before Training

Plan water intake before class. Use a plan that fits your health needs.

Source Guides for Water Before Class

Start early and sip at a steady pace.

Option A: Use Body Weight

At least 4 hours before: ACSM suggests 5 to 7 mL per kg of body weight. About 2 hours before: It suggests 3 to 5 mL per kg more if urine is dark or output is low.

Option B: Use a Set Volume

  • 2 to 3 hours before: 500 to 600 mL (17 to 20 oz) = about 2.11 to 2.54 cups
  • 10 to 20 minutes before: 200 to 300 mL (7 to 10 oz) = about 0.85 to 1.27 cups

The NATA guide gives these timing and volume ranges. Use them as a starting point to discuss your needs.

Sample Plan: Sip Before Class

This is a sample timing plan. It combines ideas from the source guides. It is a starting point to discuss with a health care pro. Choose amounts that fit your own needs.

  • 4 hours out: Use the source range that fits your needs. Sip at a steady pace.
  • 90 to 60 minutes out: Sip at a pace that feels comfortable.
  • 20 minutes out: Optional top-off of 7-10 oz if you tolerate it
  • Just before class: Ask a health care pro how to adjust intake if your stomach feels full.

Match Intake to Your Needs

Drinking too much can cause harm. The guides call for a plan that fits your needs. Avoid intake that raises your body weight right before exercise. Ask a health care pro how to plan intake.

  • Goal: meet your needs and feel comfortable.
  • If drinking raises your weight before class, ask a health care pro how to adjust the plan.
  • Notice thirst before, during, and after class.

Have a kidney, heart, blood pressure, or salt-balance condition? Ask your health care pro for a water plan before you change intake.

Simple Self-Check

  • Urine color and frequency: Track changes to discuss with a health care pro
  • One-time weigh-in test: Ask a health care pro how to estimate sweat loss from weights before and after class

FAQ

How do I drink just before class?

Try starting earlier and sipping at a steady pace. Large amounts just before class may feel uncomfortable.

What should I do about cramps?

Cramps can have more than one cause. Ask a health care pro about your needs. Keep track of sweat, fluid, and salt intake to discuss with them.

Do I need electrolytes for BJJ?

Water can meet some needs. Long, hot sessions or heavy sweat may change what you need. Ask a health care pro about fluids and salts for your training.

How do I plan water for evening class?

Use the source guides to plan ahead. Choose steady sips that fit your health needs. See current class plans.

Track Your Own Needs

Keep notes on when and how much you drink. Track how you feel in class. Discuss changes with a health care pro. See current plans.

Start with your own goals

You may want to move more or learn a new skill. You may want a group to train with. BJJ gives you mat tasks and peers to practice with.

Share your goals with Sandy before class. Tell him about old joint problems or health needs. Ask your health care team about starting a contact sport.

Learn skills one step at a time

You learn how to balance, make space, and get free. You practice with coach guidance. A new skill gives you a task to work on.

Some adults enjoy the focus and social time. Each person's response to exercise is different. Choose a pace and plan that fit you.

Ask how the coach sets the pace

BJJ is a contact sport with risks. Ask about partner matching and the first-class plan. Tell the coach what you need to train with care.

  • Learn how to tap early.
  • Release a hold when your partner taps.
  • Practice falls with coach guidance.
  • Agree on the pace with your partner.
  • Ask for a break when you need one.

Your coach can guide drills for your level. Talk with them before you try a new task. Learn the class words as you go.

Common questions after 40

Can I start at my current fitness level?

Tell Sandy what you can do now. Ask how he can adjust the drill and pace. You can work on the basics with his help.

How can I protect my joints?

Tell the coach about your health needs. Tap early and choose a controlled pace. Ask your care team about limits for your body.

What if I am older than my partners?

Ask Sandy about partner matching. Your pace, size, and skill all matter. Agree on a task that lets both people learn.

What if my range is small?

Work in a range you can control. Ask for a change to the move. Balance and timing also play a part in BJJ.

Can I ask for a calmer round?

Yes. Speak with your partner and coach. Ask for a drill or a task at your pace.

Your first class

Tour the room and learn the safety rules. Sandy guides your practice from day one. You can ask questions as you learn.

Ask what you did well and what to try next. Think about how you feel after class. Choose your next visit with time to rest.

Build a plan you can keep

Choose class times that fit your week. Leave room for rest between visits. Tell Sandy when you need to change the plan.

Early goals can be small. You might learn to tap sooner or find an escape. Watch your own progress as you practice.

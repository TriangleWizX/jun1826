Can a New Student Catch the Coach?

The goal is to build skill. Learn to attack, defend, and stay calm with a partner.

You can catch a coach in a round. The coach may give you room to try a move. Agree on the pace and goal first. A single tap tells you about that round. Reserve Free Intro to learn with a coach.

Use the round to learn what to practice next.

First, Define “Beat”

You can set a small goal for a round. Try to escape, hold a position, or stay calm.

The 5 kinds of wins that matter

  • Escape a bad spot
  • Hold position for a time
  • Keep your guard as they move
  • Apply a hold with care
  • Stay calm as your partner adds pressure

Ask your coach which skill to work on next.

The Coach Gives You Room to Work

Try a Move at Your Level

Coaches can give you room to work. You still need to choose a move and try it. The coach keeps the pace clear.

A coach helps you learn. Ask for a goal that fits your level.

Use each round to test a skill with care.

The Coach Can Add Resistance

Your coach may add more resistance as you learn. You can see where your move works and where you need help.

Practice pressure with care. Learn how to respond when a partner adds pressure.

Agree on the Round's Pace

Flow vs hard rounds

Some rounds are light; others use more resistance. Ask what pace and goal you agreed on.

Light rounds give you time to try a move. More resistance can help you check that skill.

Respect Your Partner

If you catch the coach

Respect the tap. Release the hold, reset, and thank your partner.

If the coach catches you

Ask your coach what led to the tap. Find one step to work on.

Does Beating the Coach Mean You’re Ready for Promotion?

A coach looks at skills over time. One tap is one moment. Read the belt and stripe guide for how coaches judge progress.

  • One tap is one moment
  • Ask which skills you show over time

Three Small Goals for Practice

  • Escape-only round: start in bottom side control; win = escape to guard or feet.
  • Control-only round: win = hold mount/back control for 20 seconds.
  • Decision-only round: coach gives one opening; your job is to choose the right branch.

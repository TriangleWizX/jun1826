What does BJJ sensei mean?

Sensei is an old Japanese word. In plain words, it means teacher.

The word means one who walked first. A sensei guides your steps. You learn with steady patience. You learn with gentle care.

The title shows respect for the art. It honors the path of learning.

How coach titles work

Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu blends roots from Japan and Brazil. Academies use a few friendly titles:

The title Sensei

A classic title. It honors good coaching and clear lessons.

The title Professor

The word means teacher. It is used for black belts.

The title Coach

A friendly choice for each belt rank.

Using a first name

You can call him Sandy.

Simple etiquette for your first visit

Keep titles simple and welcoming. Here is an easy guide. You can also learn common class terms.

  2. Ask with ease. Ask what title your coach prefers.
  3. Coach is a great choice. The word coach feels warm and kind.
  4. Bow as you enter. A small bow shows respect for the training mat.

Teacher 1: Kanō Jigorō

Judo Founder

Kanō built Kodokan Judo in Japan. This began in 1882. He was a wise coach. He taught balance. He taught leverage. He showed skill over force. He named his rule Jita Kyoei. This means mutual help. Kanō taught Maeda in Tokyo. He loved to teach. He helped each peer.

Line link: First teacher. He taught Maeda in Tokyo.

Read the IJF bio

Teacher 2: Mitsuyo Maeda

Judo Pioneer

Maeda was a top student of Kanō. People called him Conde Koma. He traveled the world. He shared judo. In 1914, he moved to Brazil. Maeda was skilled on the mat. He taught Carlos Gracie in Brazil. He showed ground work. He was brave and kind.

Line link: Taught by Kanō. Brought the art to Brazil. Taught Carlos Gracie.

Read the Maeda bio

Teacher 3: Carlos Gracie Sr

Gracie Art Founder

Carlos studied with Maeda in Brazil. In 1925, he opened his first school in Rio. Carlos taught his brothers. He built belt ranks. He showed students how to solve tasks on the mat. He helped kids learn. He was a great coach.

Line link: Student of Maeda. Taught brother Hélio Gracie.

Read the Carlos bio

Teacher 4: Hélio Gracie

BJJ Co-Founder

Hélio was the brother of Carlos. As a boy, he was thin and small. He changed the moves. He made them fit small bodies. He used timing and leverage. A small peer could hold a big peer. Hélio taught the next group. This included Carlos Jr.

Line link: Refined ground moves. Taught Carlos Jr.

Read the Hélio bio

Teacher 5: Carlos Gracie Jr

Gracie Barra Head

Carlos Jr is the son of Carlos Sr. He learned from his dad. He learned from his uncle Hélio. He created the Gracie Barra team in Rio. He built the world league. He taught Roberto Maia in Rio. He guided many gyms.

Line link: Built team gym systems. Coached Roberto Maia.

Read the Carlos Jr bio

Teacher 6: Roberto Maia

Boston BJJ Founder

Roberto Maia trained with Carlos Jr in Rio. Renzo Gracie gave Roberto his black belt. This was in 1994. Roberto moved to Boston. He opened Boston BJJ in 1993. He taught Kenny Florian from white belt to black belt. He was very kind.

Line link: Brought BJJ to Boston. Promoted Kenny Florian.

Read the Boston bio

Teacher 7: Kenny Florian

UFC Pro & Black Belt

Kenny Florian earned his black belt from Roberto in Boston. Kenny played soccer in college. He fought in the UFC. He fought for world belts. He spoke on television as a host. Kenny taught and promoted Josh Griffiths. He shares good skill.

Line link: Black belt from Roberto. Promoted Josh Griffiths.

Read the Kenny bio

Teacher 8: Josh Griffiths

Clockwork Coach

Josh Griffiths earned his black belt from Kenny in 2007. Josh won gold at the Pan American games. He opened Clockwork in New York City. His coaching shows how to use weight. He shows clean leverage. Josh coached Sandy for years. Josh gave Sandy his black belt in New York City.

Line link: Head coach at Clockwork. Promoted Sandy Jose Nunez.

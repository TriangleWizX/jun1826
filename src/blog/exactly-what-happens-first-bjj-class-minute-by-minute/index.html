First class guide

Your First BJJ Class: Minute-by-Minute

Your first small-group class is a coached learning experience. You will arrive, meet Sandy, learn the safety signals, move at a beginner pace, and begin with guided skill-based resistance only when the task is ready.

Fix: arrive 5-10 minutes early. Wear clean athletic clothes. Beginner-guided from the first minute.

Before Class: Map Your First Step

Training begins with a free 15-minute BJJ Goal Mapping Visit. You and Sandy discuss your goals, your weekly schedule, your starting comfort level, and the best first class lane.

Wear a clean, fitted athletic shirt with shorts, leggings, or athletic pants without zippers, buttons, or loose pockets. Bring a water bottle. Leave watches, jewelry, and street shoes off the mat.

First 60 Minutes

0-5 min

Arrive and settle in

Walk upstairs to the second-floor studio at 6045 Main Street. Sandy greets you and shows you where to place shoes, coat, water, and personal items.

5-10 min

Safety signals

Sandy teaches the most important safety tool in Jiu-Jitsu: the tap. You can tap your partner, tap the mat, or say "tap" to pause and reset.

10-20 min

Build a stable base

You practice simple movements for balance, turning, and staying organized. The goal is not athletic performance. The goal is learning how your body can move safely.

20-35 min

Follow a moving partner

You begin moving with a partner, notice what happens, and receive coaching. Sandy keeps the task narrow so you can learn without guessing.

35-50 min

Begin skill-based resistance

Your partner gives you a realistic problem to solve at a controlled pace. One person has one job. You reset whenever someone succeeds. Sandy chooses the partner, watches the activity, and adjusts resistance as your movement becomes ready.

50-60 min

Review and next step

You cool down, ask questions, and confirm which small-group class fits your schedule. Sandy helps you leave with a clear next step instead of a vague promise to come back someday.

What Skill-Based Resistance Means

Skill-based resistance is not a free-for-all. It is a coached activity with a specific position, a limited goal, and resistance chosen for your current movement. That is how beginners learn what Jiu-Jitsu feels like without being rushed into the wrong pace.

First-Class Checklist

  • Arrive 5-10 minutes early.
  • Wear clean athletic clothes without zippers, buttons, or loose pockets.
  • Bring water.
  • Remove jewelry, watches, and street shoes before stepping on the mat.
  • Tell Sandy about injuries, nerves, fitness concerns, or past training.
  • Expect coaching, pauses, resets, and a beginner-guided pace.

First Class FAQ

You can begin in clean athletic clothes. Sandy will tell you whether your selected small-group class uses a gi or no-gi clothing.

Sandy matches your partner and controls the task. Your first activities use specific positions, limited goals, and a pace selected for your starting point.

Pause, breathe, and get water. The activity can be shortened or adjusted.

Your starting fitness level helps Sandy choose the correct pace. Training develops movement, conditioning, and confidence over time.

Start With a Goal Mapping Visit

Meet Sandy for 15 minutes. Discuss your goals, map your weekly schedule, and choose the first class that fits your starting point.

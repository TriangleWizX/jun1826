The 4 Calm Defense Rules

  1. Head up tall. Spine straight.
  2. Elbows tight to ribs.
  3. Hands inside first.
  4. Sprawl with heavy hips.

1) Defensive Stance and Head Position

A good stance protects your legs. It keeps you balanced on your feet.

  • Head upright. Eyes forward.
  • Elbows tight to your ribs.
  • Hands ready to frame.

When they clinch your upper body: ear to bicep

If your partner clinches, keep your ear close to your bicep. Ask your coach to check this position. Keep your head on the side they control.

2) Hand Fighting and Elbow Positioning

Keep your hands inside. Protect your chest and neck. Good grips keep your balance safe.

Default goal: keep your hands inside. Keep your frames close.

Downblocking readiness

Downblock when your partner drops low. Move your hands down early.

The index-thumb V post

Use your hand as a post on their shoulder. Peel their grip right away. Keep your space clear.

3) Hip Pressure and Leg Positioning

When a partner grabs a leg, stay calm. Drop your weight. Drive your hips down. Move your legs back.

The sprawl is a pressure drop

  • Drive in with your hips.
  • Make your hips heavy on the mat.
  • Step your legs back with care.

Balance on the other leg

If they grab your right leg, put weight on your left leg. Counter their pull with your hips.

4) The Kick-Out and Hip Shrug

Stay facing your partner. Keep good balance on your feet. Learn class words.

  • Balance on your free leg.
  • Shrug your hip away from the grip.
  • Kick back your captured leg to break the grip.
  • Keep your chest facing forward.

Goal: clear the grip. Return to a steady stance. Learn class words.

5) Simple Frames and Resets

Great defense is simple. Take small steps to stay safe.

Reset rule: inside position

Win inside hand position. Clear their grips. Face your partner with good posture.

Practical reset steps

  • Hands come back inside.
  • Elbows return tight.
  • Head stays tall.
  • Feet reset in a solid stance.

You are back to neutral and ready again.

Common Mistakes and Quick Fixes

  • Reaching too far: keep hands close.
  • Head dropping down: keep your head high.
  • Wide elbows: keep elbows tight to ribs.
  • Sprawling late: downblock early as they drop.
  • Turning away: stay facing your partner.

3 Beginner Drills You Can Do Every Week

Hand-fight to inside position

One-minute rounds. Goal: inside hands and elbows tight the entire time.

Downblock and sprawl reaction

10 reps each side. Partner level-changes, you downblock and drop hips back.

Single-leg grip break

10 reps each side. Hip shrug plus kick-out while facing your partner.

FAQ: Quick Answers

How do I defend a single leg in BJJ?

Win inside position early. Downblock as they reach. Sprawl heavy and clear their grips.

What is the safest head position in a clinch?

Keep your head tall. Ask your coach to check your head position. Keep your ear near your bicep on the side they control.

When should I sprawl?

Sprawl as they reach for your legs. Focus on smooth timing.

How do I defend takedowns with ease?

Focus on posture. Break grips early. Keep your elbows tight.

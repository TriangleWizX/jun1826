Your breath and your pace

You can choose to slow or change your breath. Some people use breath practice to feel calmer. Effects vary from person to person.

A breath drill is a practice tool. It is separate from care for a health condition. Ask your health care team what fits your needs.

Set up with care

Use a dry, safe place to sit or lie down. Keep breath holds and fast breathing away from water. Use normal breathing in a shower, bath, or pool.

Breathe as usual when you drive or use tools. Fast breath drills and breath holds can make you faint. If you feel dizzy, sit or lie down and breathe as usual.

If you are pregnant, ask your care team first. Use their advice about breath holds and fast breathing. Heart disease, panic attacks, or seizures also call for health advice first.

Box breathing

Box breathing uses four equal parts. You breathe in, pause, breathe out, then pause again. Some people use this to find a steady pace.

You can count up to four in each part. Keep the count short enough to feel at ease. Breathe as usual if a pause feels strained.

Sit with support and relax your jaw. Let the breath feel smooth. You can choose simple slow breaths in place of holds.

The Wim Hof method

This method uses repeated deep breaths and breath holds. Fast breathing changes the gases in your blood. It can make you light-headed or make you faint.

Learn from a trained guide and follow the official method's safety guidance. Keep this practice in a dry, safe place. Ask your care team first if you have health concerns.

Use a gentle breath practice before mat work. Let your breathing return to normal before you stand. Treat fast breath work as a separate practice.

Ujjayi breathing

Ujjayi is a yoga breath with a soft throat sound. People call it an ocean breath. A trained teacher can show you how it feels.

Keep the breath soft and easy. Avoid forcing the throat or straining to make sound. You can use normal breathing while you roll.

Choose a practice that fits

For a busy moment, try slow, easy breaths. For yoga, ask a teacher about Ujjayi. For fast breath drills, follow expert safety guidance.

A breath drill may help you notice your pace. It gives you a skill to practice. It also leaves room for medical care when needed.

A week of practice

Choose a brief, gentle practice that fits your health. Sit in a safe place and breathe at ease. Notice how you feel before and after.

Keep the practice short enough to stay at ease. Ask a teacher if you have questions. You can talk with Sandy about breathing during coached drills.

Study facts

Jiu-Jitsu gives you live problems to solve.

Your partner moves. You choose what to do next. Then you adjust. Studies can help us ask good questions. They cannot tell us what each person will gain.

How we teach

Games help you learn by doing.

These studies look at ways to learn through play, movement, and choice. Some are about BJJ. Others are about school PE or ideas for teachers.

BJJ study · 2026 · 60 adult athletes

Practice with live choices

One group trained with changing tasks. Some strength, light-response, and stress scores changed more in that group.

Limit: The study did not test live grappling skill or later recall. It did not test kids or new learners.

Read the BJJ study

Review of studies · other sports · 2024 online / 2025 issue

Learning through games

A review of 28 studies with 1,600 people found better game choices and some skill-test scores with game-based teaching.

Limit: The results did not show better skill during games or more knowledge. These were not BJJ studies.

Read the review

Review of studies · school PE · 2026

Game teaching can have mixed results

A review looked at 17 school PE studies. Fewer than half showed clear gains in movement skill.

Limit: The studies used different methods. They do not show that one way works best for all. They did not study BJJ.

Read the review

Expert ideas · 2026 · not a trial

Set a fair challenge

Two authors say tasks should fit the learner and the moment. They call for checks of learning over time.

Limit: This is an expert view. It is not a test of a BJJ class or proof that one method works.

What BJJ may help

What these studies found

BJJ study

BJJ and school behavior

In one school study, some teacher-rated scores changed more after 12 weeks of BJJ than PE.

Who: 88 grade-six boys joined; 80 finished.

Limit: This one study cannot show that BJJ helps each child focus.

Study details

Other sport · Taekwondo

Taekwondo and focus

A study of 240 children ages 7 to 11 found changes in some focus and behavior scores.

Limit: This studied Taekwondo, not BJJ. It cannot show that BJJ has the same result.

Study details

Review of studies · other sports

Sports with changing play

A review of 17 studies included 1,298 young people. It found changes in some thinking scores.

Limit: The review covered many sports. It cannot show that BJJ or our school is best.

Review details

BJJ study · adults

BJJ as exercise

In a small study, some fitness scores changed after six weeks of BJJ classes.

16 adults new to training took part.

Limit: This small study cannot show that BJJ beats other exercise or change each person’s fitness.

Study details

BJJ and MMA study

Self-control and kind acts

In a five-month study, both groups said some scores went up. Aggression scores changed in different ways.

113 people ages 15 to 24 finished. They chose their sport.

Limit: The study cannot show that a sport caused the changes.

In class

Learn one step at a time.

These are class examples at Sensei Sandy BJJ. The studies above did not test these exact games.

  1. Learn a safe way to move.
  2. Try it with a partner who matches your size and skill.
  3. Use light, coached resistance.
  4. Try the skill in a short round.

Chest to chest

One partner holds balance. The other works to make space, get guard, or stand.

Chest to back

One partner works to escape. The other practices calm control.

Guard

One partner works to pass. The other keeps space, sweeps, or stands.

What studies cannot promise

These studies cannot tell us what one student will gain. They do not test every class or every way to teach. We cannot promise a change in focus, grades, fitness, or mood.

Family Guides & Mat Goals

Local BJJ Tournaments for Families

Competition is an optional adventure. We treat tournaments as team road trips focused on courage, fun, and learning.

A Healthy View of Competition

Entering a tournament is always a personal choice for each student and family. On our team, tournaments are celebrations of effort. We celebrate stepping onto the mat, trying your techniques with unfamiliar partners, and traveling together as a Catskills team.

How We Prepare in Class

When students choose to compete, Coach Sandy guides them through practice rounds at the studio. Kids and teens learn the scoring rules, how to listen to their coach from the edge of the mat, and how to reset with a calm breath between matches.

Planning Your Family Tournament Day

Regional youth and adult tournaments take place throughout the Hudson Valley and Capital Region, including Albany, Kingston, and Newburgh. Here are simple tips to keep the day smooth and fun:

  • Pack Simple Essentials: Bring two clean uniforms, plenty of water, healthy fruit, and comfortable team slip-on sandals.
  • Arrive Early: Give your child plenty of time to check in, walk around the venue, and warm up with teammates.
  • Focus on One Skill: Pick one move to practice during the day, such as securing a strong guard or hitting a smooth sweep single.

The Post-Match Celebration

Whatever happens on the scoreboard, every tournament finishes with smiles. Our team tradition is gathering for burgers and ice cream on the drive home. We talk about what went well, share laughs, and return to Monday class with fresh curiosity.

Frequently Asked Questions for Parents

Are students required to compete?

Every student trains for their own goals, whether that means physical fitness, calm self-defense, or simply enjoying active time with friends. Competition is strictly optional.

How are youth divisions organized?

Youth brackets are carefully matched by exact age, weight, and belt rank so children compete alongside peers with similar experience.

Will a coach be matside with my child?

Yes. Coach Sandy or an assistant coach stands right at the edge of the mat to guide your child with calm, supportive advice throughout their match.

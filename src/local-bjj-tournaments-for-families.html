Mat Philosophy

A Healthy View of Competition

We keep tournament days relaxed and supportive. Entering an event is a personal choice that belongs to each student and family.

Personal Pace

Always an Individual Choice

Stepping onto the tournament mat is always an individual choice. Students decide when they feel excited to test their skills. Many students train happily for recreation, balance, and cooperative health.

Courage & Poise

Courage with New Partners

Effort, courage, and trying techniques with unfamiliar partners are celebrated. Moving with a new peer teaches adaptable balance, spatial awareness, and calm composure under gentle pressure.

Equal Support

Equal High-Fives for All

Wins and lessons receive the exact same warm high-fives and coach encouragement. Every round delivers clear feedback, fresh confidence, and shared team smiles on the ride home.

Mat Day Prep

Family Tournament Day Checklist

A calm game day starts with thoughtful gear packing. Here are four essentials every Catskills family packs for smooth tournament travel.

Checklist 01

Two Clean Gis

Pack two clean gis, or a gi plus no-gi rashguards if entering both divisions. A backup uniform keeps your child fresh and comfortable across multiple rounds.

Checklist 02

Team Slip-on Sandals

Clean footwear when stepping off the mats. Slip-on shoes maintain clean feet in common walkways and restrooms before walking back onto the competition surface.

Checklist 03

Fresh Fruit & Water

Hydration and easily digestible snacks between matches. Cold water, crisp apple slices, and bananas provide gentle energy during bracket intervals.

Checklist 04

One Focused Skill

Pick one move or position to focus on for the day (e.g. guard retention or sweep single). Concentrating on a single technical task keeps the student calm and centered.

Coaching & Respect

Matside Coaching & Etiquette

Respect for the referee, opponent, and mat culture shapes every step of the day. Students learn to handle new challenges with grace.

Coach Guidance

Dedicated Matside Coach

Coach Sandy or an assistant coach stands right at the edge of the mat to guide your child with calm, supportive advice throughout their match. Clear reminders help kids stay composed and breathe.

Safety Culture

Tapping Early as Honor

Tapping early is honored as the foundation of sportsmanship and personal safety. Tapping communicates respect between athletes and allows students to reset and learn with total peace of mind.

Mutual Respect

Handshakes Before & After

Handshake with referee and opponent before and after each match. We thank our opponents for testing our balance and helping us grow as martial artists.

Parent Inquiries

Frequently Asked Questions for Parents

Clear answers about tournament participation, child safety, and mat etiquette.

Are students required to compete? +

Strictly optional. Every student trains for individual goals, such as personal fitness, quiet self-defense, or joyful physical play with friends. Families decide whether competition fits their schedule and interests.

How are youth divisions organized? +

Youth brackets are organized by exact age, weight, and belt rank. This thoughtful grouping ensures children test their skills against peers of similar size and mat experience.

Will a coach be matside with my child? +

Yes. Coach Sandy or an assistant coach stands right at the edge of the mat to guide your child with calm, supportive advice throughout their match.

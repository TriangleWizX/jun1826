The Road Trip and the Podium Goal

A Fun Family Adventure

Pack the car with great snacks and road trip tunes. We drive down the mountain together. We walk in with our team. Our gis are clean and bright. We cheer for our friends on the mats all day long.

Aim for the Top of the Podium

When we step on the mat, our goal is clear. Our job is to make it to the top of the podium! We train hard in class. We give our best work in every round. We make sure we did all we could to earn that top spot.

Burgers and Ice Cream After

After every match, short or long, win or learn, Coach Sandy is right there with a high five and cold water. Then we leave together for big burgers and ice cream! We celebrate as one big team.

Pre-Tournament Preparation

Family Tournament Checklist +

Check these simple steps on the official page before you register:

  • Right age group: Events group by birth year or current age.
  • Right belt and rank: Check match round lengths for your division.
  • Realistic weight group: Check weigh-in clothes rules.
  • Gi and No-Gi divisions: Pick the styles you practice in class.
  • Weigh-in details: Check arrival times and weight rules.
  • Credit policy: Check if they offer credit if your plans shift.
  • Schedule updates: Brackets usually post two days before match day.
  • Travel and parking: Pick a great spot to park early.
What to Pack in Your Bag +

Pack your gear bag the night before for an easy, calm morning:

  • Clean Gi and belt: Fresh, dry, and ready for action.
  • Slide sandals: Wear these when you step off the mat.
  • Water bottle: Stay cool and fresh all day.
  • Simple snacks: Fresh fruit, pretzels, and granola bars.
  • Warm team hoodie: Big gym rooms can feel cool between rounds.
  • Phone charger: Keep your phone charged for mat calls.
  • Books or music: Rest well between rounds.

What a tournament can teach

A tournament is an optional testing environment. It is a fun challenge to test your skills. In our school, students advance through steady practice and showing up.

After each match, we talk about what went well. We pick new skills to practice in class next week.

  1. Does the student want this experience?
  2. What skills would we like to practice under pressure?
  3. Is the event match setup a great fit for the student?
  4. What would we like to learn, regardless of the result?

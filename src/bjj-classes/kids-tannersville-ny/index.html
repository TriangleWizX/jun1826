Kids program overview

Meet Sensei Sandy

Your child starts in a calm, structured room where every drill is coached, every partner is matched thoughtfully, and parents can see the process.

Hard enough to learn. Safe enough to keep trying. Sandy adjusts the task, partner, and resistance so your child can work through a real problem without having the answer supplied. We make the problem appropriate without making it fake.

What parents can notice

What parents can see

The goal is not a vague promise to “build confidence.” It is helping your child handle small, real moments with more control. Want to see how Sandy turns those observations into progress feedback? See the Progress Report Card.

What they are practicing over time

Listen → Reset → Cooperate → Try Again

Listen and reset

Return attention after getting distracted and follow the next coached step.

Stop on a tap

Recognize a partner’s signal, stop immediately, and restart with care.

Ask for help

Name what feels confusing instead of panicking or pretending to understand.

Handle a hard round

Work through frustration, solve one problem, and try again with coached resistance.

Around here, the rules are simple: listen, keep going when the problem is hard, and take care of your training partner. When they can, kids try their own answer before asking for one.

What research can tell us

Structured martial arts can ask attention to move with the problem.

Why live problems matter: research on open-skill sports—activities where conditions change and participants must continually adjust—has reported positive effects on selected executive-function measures in children and adolescents. BJJ shares that changing-problem structure through partner movement and controlled resistance. Individual outcomes vary; this research describes populations, not a guarantee for your child.

What Happens During Class?

Students do not spend class watching long demonstrations or repeating movements without resistance.

Each class progresses through:

  1. Movement connected to the day’s problem
  2. Chest-to-chest control and escapes
  3. Chest-to-back control and escapes
  4. A neutral guard game
  5. Coach-controlled resistance rounds
  6. A brief review

Sandy changes the partners, starting positions, scoring rules, and resistance level so beginners and experienced students can learn safely.

What parents can look for over time

Reset Attention

Gets distracted, returns attention, and follows the next coached instruction.

Take Care of a Partner

Stops on a tap, follows the agreed rules, and gives their partner a safe problem to solve.

Try Again After Difficulty

Gets stopped, resets, and tries another useful solution instead of treating the mistake as the end of the round.

Speak Up When Something Is Wrong

Asks a question, tells the coach when something feels wrong, and uses tapping or words to set a clear boundary.

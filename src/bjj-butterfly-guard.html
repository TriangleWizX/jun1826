What is BJJ butterfly guard?

By Sandy Jose Nunez · Updated October 25, 2026

Reserve Free Intro

The guard keeps you safe. It protects you from below. The bjj butterfly guard is a seated guard. You place your feet inside. Your feet act like hooks. These hooks lift the top player. You use body leverage. You win with balance.

What butterfly guard looks like

You sit up on the mat. Your back stays tall. Your knees open wide. You slip your feet inside. You hook the partner's thighs.

These spots are called butterfly hooks. A hook lifts your partner. It tilts them with ease. Your hands stay active. You protect your space. You stay balanced and calm.

What butterfly guard is for

Butterfly guard helps make space. It does three main jobs.

  • Tipping base. When a partner stays heavy, your hooks lift them. You shift their weight.
  • Sweeps. A butterfly sweep tilts your partner. You come up on top.
  • Angle shifts. You slide your hips. You drag the arm. You take the back.

When do beginners learn it?

New students learn basics first. You practice posture and base. You learn side escapes.

In our adult White Belt Foundations cycle, butterfly guard comes later in the course.

  • Lesson 16: Sweep with a butterfly hook. You lift with a hook. You tilt your partner safely.
  • Lesson 18: Guard attack with butterfly. You drag the arm. You take the back.

This clear path builds good habits first.

Common mistakes to avoid

Butterfly guard takes practice. Here is one common slip.

A common mistake with butterfly guard is using it passively instead of with structure and timing. If you lie flat, hooks lose lift. Keep your chest up. Sit tall on the mat. Sandy cues in class: Stay calm and keep your structure.

Related guards to explore

Butterfly guard is an open guard. Explore these spots in our dictionary.

  • Guard: The core spot where legs keep you safe.
  • Closed Guard: Legs wrap around the waist.
  • Open Guard: Feet stay free to block moves.
  • Half Guard: Trapping one leg between yours.
  • Seated Guard: Sitting tall to manage space.
  • De La Riva: Hooking a standing leg from outside.

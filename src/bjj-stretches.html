Beginner BJJ Mobility Guide

BJJ Stretches for Beginners

Easy moves for kids, teens, adults, and parents before BJJ class. This short plan takes 5 to 10 minutes.

Why move before class?

BJJ uses your hips, back, arms, legs, and neck. These easy moves help you get set for class.

Move slow. Take easy breaths. Stop if you feel pain. Then you can learn with care.

Safety note: Move with care. Stay in a range that feels good. Ask your doctor if you have health needs before you try new moves.

The 5 to 10 Minute BJJ Mobility Routine

Do each move 5 to 10 times. Go slow. Keep breathing as you move.

1

Hip Circles

Stand tall with feet apart. Move your hips in a small circle. Go both ways.

This helps you move on the mat and get up.

2

Pelvic Tilts

Bend your knees a bit. Tip your hips front, back, and side to side.

This helps you feel your back and hips before class.

3

Forward Fold to Stand

Bend at your hips. Let your back rest. Stand up slow.

This helps wake up your back and legs.

4

Side Bends

Lift one arm. Lean to the other side. Then switch.

This helps your sides and arms get set for class.

5

Shoulder Rolls

Lift your shoulders, roll them back, then down. Go both ways.

This helps your arms and shoulders get set.

6

Arm Circles

Make small arm circles. Go both ways.

This helps your arms get warm.

7

Slow Neck Turns

Turn your head left and right. Look up and down. Move slow.

This helps you check how your neck feels.

8

Flat-Foot Squats

Keep your heels down if you can. Bend your knees. Stand up slow.

This helps you bend and stand with care.

9

World’s Easiest Lunge

Step one foot back. Reach up. Take a breath. Switch sides.

This helps your hips move with ease.

10

Cat-Cow

Get on your hands and knees. Round your back, then lift it. Breathe.

This helps your back get set for mat work.

11

Downward Dog to Plank

Move slow from a light V shape to a plank. Keep it smooth.

This uses your arms, hips, legs, and belly.

12

Technical Stand-Up Reps

Sit down. Put one hand back. Cover your face with the other. Step back and stand.

This is a BJJ move to help you stand up with care.

Before class vs after class

Before class

Use easy moves. Try hips, arms, back, squats, and safe ways to stand.

After class

Move slow if your body feels warm. Keep each stretch easy. Keep breathing.

First class tip

You can get more limber with practice. We shape each class to fit you.

Best BJJ stretches for tight adults

Tight hips

Hip circles, easy lunges, and flat-foot squats.

Tight shoulders

Shoulder rolls, arm circles, and cat-cow.

Tight lower back

Pelvic tilts, cat-cow, and gentle forward folds.

Nervous before class

Breathe deep, move with control, and arrive 10 minutes early.

For parents: should kids stretch before BJJ?

Kids need less stretch time than adults. They can crawl, squat, balance, and learn to fall with care.

Coaches lead kids through warm-ups. Parents can help at home: pack water, use the bath, and get here calm.

BJJ Stretching FAQ

Do I need to be flexible before starting BJJ?

You do not need to be limber to start. New students learn with calm help and easy moves.

Should I stretch before or after Jiu-Jitsu?

Before class, move with care. After class, slow stretches may feel good when you are warm.

What should I keep in mind before BJJ?

Move slow in a range that feels good. The goal is to feel ready for class.

Can kids use this routine?

Yes. Keep it simple: hip circles, squats, cat-cow, and safe ways to stand.

Can I do this at home?

Yes. Try it at home or before class. You need a small space and a few minutes.

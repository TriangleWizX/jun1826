SANDY JOSÉ NÚÑEZ · BLACK BELT COACH · TANNERSVILLE, NY

I'm Sandy Nunez. I coach jiu-jitsu in Tannersville.

I teach Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu for kids, teens, and adults on Main Street in Tannersville. My focus is on calm, structured coaching where beginners can learn safely and build skill without intimidation.

Your first class is a coached learning experience. Whether you have never stepped on a mat before or you are returning to training after years away, we start at the right pace from day one.

How I got here

Different rooms taught me to see the person in front of me.

I started teaching private lessons and kids at Clockwork Jiu Jitsu in Manhattan. Later, I ran the Adult BJJ program at Physical Culture Collective in Brooklyn.

When I moved upstate, I trained at Oneonta Jiu Jitsu and taught some classes there. Then I spent months teaching at Firehouse Jiu Jitsu during its early days in Catskill. I taught seminars at Iron Guard Dojo in Pine Bush, and later served as Head Jiu Jitsu Instructor at Ascended Athletics in Kingston.

I was awarded my black belt by Josh Griffiths at Clockwork Jiu Jitsu.

Teaching across different academies and working with different students shaped how I coach today. I became far less interested in forcing everyone to move identically, and much more interested in understanding the person standing in front of me.

Academy Origins

Why I built Sensei Sandy BJJ

Before there was a permanent academy, I taught wherever the mats fit: a garage in Prattsville, a friend's lawn in Woodstock, Boot Camp Gym space in Hurley, and a storage facility in Hunter where the mats had to come back up when training was over.

In August 2025, Sensei Sandy BJJ got a room of its own on Main Street in Tannersville. The space gave our mountaintop community a dedicated home for year-round martial arts training.

I built the academy around personal coaching rather than packed classes. Keeping class sizes small ensures that students do not disappear into the background. I want to know who you are partnering with, what technique you are working on, and how you are progressing each week.

Why I teach this way

Beginners should be coached, not tested.

You should not have to prove toughness before someone agrees to coach you. Embarrassment is never the price of admission on our mats.

When someone is new, I choose their training partner thoughtfully. We make the immediate problem clear, control the level of resistance, and adjust the starting position to match where you are. If a drill feels overwhelming, we simplify it. When you find a solution that works for your body, we build from there.

Skill-based resistance activities begin at the right pace from day one because Jiu-Jitsu must work against real movement. But we never use intimidation or chaos as teaching methods. My goal is to help you understand the mechanics and make calm, effective decisions of your own.

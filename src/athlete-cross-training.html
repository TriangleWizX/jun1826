Catskills athlete cross-training

Keep Your Sport. Add One Mat Hour.

Jiu-jitsu gives youth and adult athletes another place to practice balance, contact, ground recovery and decision-making against controlled resistance. Your primary sport stays primary.

Map Your First Class View the Mostly No-Gi Schedule

Who this track serves

Athletes from soccer and basketball, running and cross-country, skiing and snowboarding, hiking and cycling, golf and racquet sports, and strength training can use one coached mat hour as complementary movement practice.

⚽ 🏃 ⛷️ 🚲 ⛳ Youth and adult athletes are welcome to ask about fit.

What happens during class

Expect a coached warm-up, skill practice, and carefully paced resistance. The goal is a broader movement experience involving balance, contact, ground recovery, and decisions—not a promise of competitive results.

Why the fall schedule is mostly No-Gi

Youth train No-Gi Monday through Wednesday, with Friday Gi Lab. Adults train No-Gi Monday and Wednesday, Gi Tuesday and Friday, plus Saturday No-Gi. The format is shown before you arrive so clothing is easy to plan.

Youth and adult pathways

Most local beginners enter Core Culture. In-season athletes who can realistically attend about once weekly may qualify for a flexible athlete track after Goal Mapping. Ask Sandy about the right fit; the athlete option does not replace the main Core Culture pathway.

Review visitor and flexible-access options

Evidence and limitations

This is complementary movement practice, not medical care or a performance guarantee. Sensei Sandy does not claim that jiu-jitsu prevents injuries, makes athletes faster, or guarantees confidence, fitness, or competitive results.

Cross-training FAQs

Do I need a rank rashguard?

No. Beginners do not need special equipment for a No-Gi first class. Clean fitted athletic clothing is enough.

Can I start with one class weekly?

Possibly. Start with a Free Intro and Goal Mapping so Sandy can confirm the best class and pathway for your schedule.

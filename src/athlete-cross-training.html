90-day Second Sport Lab pilot · Catskills athlete cross-training

Keep Your Sport. Add One Mat Hour.

Jiu-jitsu gives youth and adult athletes another place to practice balance, contact, ground recovery and decision-making against controlled resistance. Your primary sport stays primary.

Map Your First Class View the Complete Gi + No-Gi Schedule

Who this track serves

Athletes from soccer and basketball, running and cross-country, skiing and snowboarding, hiking and cycling, golf and racquet sports, and strength training can use one coached mat hour as complementary movement practice.

⚽ 🏃 ⛷️ 🚲 ⛳ Youth and adult athletes are welcome to ask about fit.

What happens during class

Expect a coached warm-up, skill practice, and carefully paced resistance. Practice may include balance and base under contact, controlled falling and returning to the feet, hip/chest/knee positioning, frames, grips, posture, scaled live decision-making, and responsible training-load awareness—not a promise of competitive results.

Two low-gear entry days for athletes

Youth train No-Gi Monday and Wednesday, Gi Tuesday and Friday. Adults train No-Gi Monday and Wednesday, Gi Tuesday and Friday, plus Saturday No-Gi. Cross-training students can start once weekly in athletic clothes on Monday or Wednesday, while Core Culture students receive a complete Gi and No-Gi curriculum.

Youth and adult pathways

Most local beginners enter Core Culture. In-season athletes who can realistically attend about once weekly may qualify for the existing Athlete Cross-Training Track: 10 visits for $300 after Goal Mapping. Athletes expecting two or more weekly BJJ classes should move into Core Culture; the athlete option does not replace the main pathway.

Evidence and limitations

This is complementary movement practice, not medical care or a performance guarantee. Sensei Sandy does not claim that jiu-jitsu prevents injuries, makes athletes faster, or guarantees confidence, fitness, or competitive results. Read the parent evidence and limitations handout.

Cross-training FAQs

Do I need a rank rashguard?

No. Beginners do not need special equipment for a No-Gi first class. Clean fitted athletic clothing is enough.

Can I start with one class weekly?

Possibly. Start with a Free Intro and Goal Mapping so Sandy can confirm the best class and pathway for your schedule.

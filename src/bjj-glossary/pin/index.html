Basics · Beginner

Pin

Quick meaning

In Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, a pin is a top position that limits a partner’s movement with body control.

In plain words

You stay on top and use your body position to hold control. Side control and mount are common pinning positions.

Why it helps

A pin can help you hold a position while you look for the next safe step. Good control comes from balance, position, and coach cues.

Start here

Learn how to hold position and let a partner move. Ask your coach to guide the drill.

What a pin can teach

A pin helps you learn how top and bottom spots work. The top person uses body shape to stay set. The person on the bottom can move, make space, and work toward a new spot. Both people have a task. A coach sets the task and pace.

In class, you may start in side control or mount. Your coach shows where hands, knees, or hips can go. Each move has a clear goal. You can try to hold the spot for a short count. Then your partner can move and find space. Take turns so each person gets to learn both roles.

Good pinning uses balance and care. Keep your weight set by your coach’s cue. Use a light touch and watch your partner. A calm hold fits the task. Follow the coach’s cue. Give your partner room to move. Your partner can tap or ask for a reset. Both people can then start again with a new cue.

Side control and mount are two common pins. Your coach can show how they differ. Ask what to notice first. You may learn to keep your base, move your hips, or follow a turn. One cue at a time can help you see how the spot changes.

Coach says

Ask your coach to show you.

Stay safe

Move with control and communicate early while training pin.

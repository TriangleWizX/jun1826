The Kimura

What is the kimura?

The kimura is a figure-four shoulder lock in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu. It controls the wrist and upper arm, then guides the arm behind the back to turn the shoulder joint. People use the kimura in Gi and No-Gi Jiu-Jitsu.

In plain words

Hold the wrist, bend the arm, and use a figure-four grip to guide the shoulder. A coach can show the grip and the safe range of motion.

In Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, the kimura can help you hold a position, make a sweep, or move to another hold.

How the kimura works

A good kimura uses control and angle, not force. One arm bends and holds your partner's arm. Your other arm joins it to make a figure-four grip.

The kimura can help you hold a position, sweep, or move to another hold. You may see it from guard, side control, or north-south.

Start here

Move slow. Let go when your partner taps.

Coach cue: Stay calm and keep your structure while working kimura.

Coach says

Keep the figure-four grip and move with care. In Gi and No-Gi class, a coach can show how to set up the kimura and guide each step.

Stay safe

Move with care. Let go when your partner taps.

Coached rounds begin at a controlled pace so everyone trains safely.

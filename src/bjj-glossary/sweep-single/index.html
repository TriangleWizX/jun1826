Sweep Single Leg Takedown in BJJ

A sweep single is a takedown entry that circles to the outside of your partner's lead leg.

Quick Meaning

In grappling, a sweep single is an angular variation of the single-leg takedown. Rather than driving directly into your partner, you drop your level, secure the lead leg, and rotate your hips around the corner to compromise their base.

In Plain Words

You step to the outside of your training partner's foot, cup their leg with both arms, and circle like you are sweeping the mat with a broom. This gentle pivot takes their balance without requiring heavy impact.

Why the Sweep Single Helps in Jiu-Jitsu

Driving straight forward into a partner can sometimes expose your neck to headlocks or guillotines. The sweep single keeps your head positioned tightly against their ribs on the outside, creating mechanical protection while opening clean passing angles once you reach the ground.

Key Steps for Beginners

  • Setup: Use hand-fighting or collar ties to induce a small step forward from your partner.
  • Level Change: Bend your knees and drop your hips before stepping in, keeping your spine upright.
  • Corner Turn: Pivot your rear leg around the side to steer their weight over their heel.
  • Controlled Finish: Lower your partner to the mats with support rather than slamming.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the sweep single safe for beginners?

Yes. Because the sweep single works through rotational leverage and angles rather than high-impact lifting, it is one of the safest takedowns to learn first.

Do I have to do standing takedowns in my first class?

No. New students begin on the mat learning ground positions and basic defense. Standing concepts are introduced when you have mastered comfortable mat movement.

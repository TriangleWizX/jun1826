Hip Heist

Quick meaning

A hip heist is a movement where a student quickly rotates the hips out from underneath and turns the body to face a new angle, often to build base, come up, or escape pressure.

In plain words

Hip heist means swinging the hips out so you can turn and get into a stronger position fast.

In Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, understanding how hip heist connects to balance and leverage helps you make smart choices during partner drills.

Why it helps

A hip heist is a movement where a student quickly rotates the hips out from underneath and turns the body to face a new angle, often to build base, come up, or escape pressure.

Hip heists help students connect ground movement to wrestling-style motion. They are useful for building up from bottom. changing angle during scrambles. and turning defense into movement.

Practicing this movement gives you confidence during partner movement. You learn when to build frames, when to create space, and how to stay calm.

Start here

A common mistake is moving the hips before posting firmly on the hand or foot, which makes the motion collapse.

FAQ

Why is hip heist useful for beginners?

It links base-building and angle changes so students can get up or move out of pressure more effectively.

What makes hip heist fail most often?

Weak posting on hand or foot, which causes the hips to collapse during the turn.

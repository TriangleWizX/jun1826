Class Breakdown

Your reservation occupies a 60-minute schedule window. Approximately 45 minutes contain structured training. The surrounding time allows students to enter calmly, change groups, ask questions, collect their belongings, and leave without rushing the next class.

Class segment Time
Arrival and partner match 3 minutes
Useful movement 5 minutes
Chest-to-chest game 6 minutes
Chest-to-back game 6 minutes
Neutral guard game 6 minutes
Resistance rounds 15 minutes
Review and room reset 4 minutes
Total coached class 45 minutes

How We Organize Learning

  1. Position: Where are we?
  2. Problem: What is the other person stopping us from doing?
  3. Principle: What has to become true?
  4. Options: What useful choices are available?
  5. Resistance: Can the solution survive a partner trying to stop it?
  6. Adaptation: What changes when the first answer fails?

Teach the idea. Coach the person.

A Beginner's Four-Part Map

  • Standing: Stay balanced. Find an advantage.
  • Top: Stay on top. Improve control.
  • Bottom: Stay safe. Recover your legs or get up.
  • Back: Protect your neck and control the back.

Class Timeline

03 min

Arrival and Partner Match

Students enter, settle in, and receive a suitable partner assigned based on age, size, experience, and temperament.

05 min

Useful Movement

Students explore 2–3 movements such as bridging, stepping over, or turning toward pressure that may help solve the problems later in class.

06 min

Chest-to-Chest Connection

Bottom student works toward recovering guard or standing; top student works toward holding control.

06 min

Chest-to-Back Control

Defending student escapes to neutral position; attacking student works toward maintaining chest-to-back control.

06 min

Neutral Guard Game

Top student controls space; bottom student recovers guard, reverses position, or stands.

15 min

Resistance Rounds

Short positional rounds connecting positions with coached resistance at the right pace.

04 min

Review and Room Reset

Students review key escape movements, thank partners, and conclude class.

Your Partner Makes the Lesson Real

A partner is not there to let a technique happen. Each student has a job, and resistance makes the problem honest. Partners regulate one another's safety so both people can test decisions, reset when needed, and keep the round productive.

Beginner Lane

A first-day student does not need every technique or every rule.

The Beginner Lane gives them:

  • One selected partner
  • One safety rule
  • One useful movement
  • One scoring target

A beginner first learns how to tap, stop, protect the neck, bridge, and move with controlled resistance.

They remain part of the class instead of being isolated from it. Sandy reduces the number of choices until the student feels comfortable solving the problem.

Brief Demonstrations. More Guided Practice.

Each explanation follows the same sequence:

  1. See the starting position.
  2. Hear each student’s job.
  3. Watch one useful action.
  4. Learn how to score.
  5. Begin the round.

Most demonstrations remain under 45 seconds.

Sandy corrects individual students while the room continues moving. He stops the full group only when several pairs need the same correction.

More Skill Means More Choices

Beginners receive smaller problems and more guidance. As students recognize situations more reliably, Sandy widens the problem, asks better questions, and removes prompts so more than one useful solution can appear.

The goal is not for a student to remember what Sandy would do. It is for the student to recognize what is happening, choose a useful response, and adjust when the partner changes. Sandy still manages safety, partner matching, task design, and appropriate resistance; what gradually decreases is the need for him to supply every technical answer.

What Parents Can Expect

Parents may watch youth classes. During class, Sandy manages partner assignments, training intensity, physical safety, disruptive pairings, beginner pacing, and submission readiness.

After class, parents may receive one specific observation about progress, effort, listening, partner care, or an area that needs support. Those observations can be carried into the Progress Report Card so families can see what is becoming reliable.

The goal is not to keep children busy for an hour. The goal is to give them repeated opportunities to move, think, cooperate, and make better decisions under manageable pressure.

Why We Teach This Way

A student should not need the coach to solve every problem forever. We narrow the problem enough that you can begin, give you resistance that makes the answer matter, and gradually give you more freedom as your judgment improves.

What Students Review

Class ends with one practical question:

  • Which movement helped you escape?
  • How did you maintain the connection?
  • Which space did you need to protect?
  • What will you try during the next class?

Students thank their partners, collect their belongings, and help check the mat area.

Unfinished material carries into the next class. Students do not lose training time because the coach tries to force every planned technique into one session.

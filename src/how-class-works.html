Why each round is new

In Jiu-Jitsu, your partner moves and changes the pace. You cannot plan each move in advance. Studies on sports with changing tasks report mental benefits for youth. Read the studies and their limits in our evidence guide.

Read our evidence guide and its limits.

Class times

Each visit has a 60-minute time slot. About 45 minutes are coach-led training. The extra time lets you enter, ask questions, change, and pack up.

A four-part map for new students

  • Standing: Stay balanced. Find an advantage.
  • Top: Stay on top. Improve control.
  • Bottom: Stay safe. Recover your legs or get up.
  • Back: Protect your neck and control the back.

How Sandy coaches new students

Sandy keeps each round clear and safe.

  • Position: Start from a clear place.
  • Problem: Know what your partner is defending.
  • Key idea: Learn what must happen.
  • Options: Try one useful choice.
  • Resistance: Work with a partner at a safe pace.
  • Change: Try a new task if the first move fails.

Teach the idea. Coach the person.

Class steps

03 min

Arrival and Partner Match.

Students come in and settle. Sandy pairs them by age, size, and skill.

05 min

Useful Movement.

Students try 2 to 3 basic moves. They bridge, step over, and turn with care.

06 min

Chest-to-Chest Connection.

The student on bottom tries to get guard or stand. The student on top works to stay balanced and in control.

06 min

Chest-to-Back Control.

One student tries to get free. The other uses gentle control from behind.

06 min

Neutral Guard Game.

One student tries to pass the guard. The other keeps space, sweeps, or stands.

15 min

Resistance Rounds.

Short games link each skill. Sandy keeps the pace calm and safe.

04 min

Review and Room Reset.

Students review ways to get free. All thank their partners and tidy the mats.

Your partner helps you learn

Each student has a clear job. A partner gives you a real task. Both care for each other. You try a choice, take a break, and try again.

New student class and rules

A first-day student starts with one clear task, one useful movement, and guided practice.

The Beginner class gives them:

  • One selected partner.
  • One safety rule.
  • One useful movement.
  • One scoring target.

New students learn to tap, let go, guard the neck, bridge, and move with care. We review published training research (such as the 2025 global BJJ injury study) to guide our room structure and partner safety rules.

Students join the group on day one. Sandy keeps each task simple.

Short demos. More practice.

Each demo has these steps:

  1. See the starting position.
  2. Hear each student’s job.
  3. Watch one useful action.
  4. Learn how to score.
  5. Begin the round.

Most demos last less than 45 seconds.

Sandy gives tips as students work. He stops the group if many pairs need the same tip.

More Skill Means More Choices

New students start with a clear task and steady help. As you learn each position, Sandy can add another choice.

Learn the task, pick a move, and adjust as your partner moves. Sandy sets the pairs and pace.

What Parents Can Expect

Parents may watch youth classes. Sandy guides partner matches, practice pace, and room safety.

After class, Sandy can share notes on effort, listening, and partner care. See the Progress Report Card.

Kids move, think, and work with a partner as a coach guides them.

Why We Teach This Way

Students learn by doing. Each task has a clear goal. Sandy adds safe resistance as they are ready and helps them change their moves.

What Students Review

At the end of class, students think about a question:

  • Which movement helped you escape?
  • How did you hold the position?
  • Which space did you need to protect?
  • What will you try in the next class?

Students thank their partners, get their gear, and help tidy the mat.

Why changing conditions matter.

Jiu-Jitsu is an open-skill practice. Your partner changes position, pressure, and timing. The answers cannot be set ahead of time. Studies on open-skill sports show good mental benefits for youth. We share those studies in our evidence guide. They help explain our active learning model.

Class Breakdown.

Each visit occupies a 60-minute reserved window. About 45 minutes are coach-led training. The extra time lets students enter calmly. You can ask questions, change clothes, and pack up at your own pace.

A Beginner's Four-Part Map.

  • Standing: Stay balanced. Find an advantage.
  • Top: Stay on top. Improve control.
  • Bottom: Stay safe. Recover your legs or get up.
  • Back: Protect your neck and control the back.

How Sandy coaches beginners.

Sandy keeps each round clear and safe.

  • Position: Start from a clear place.
  • Problem: Know what your partner is defending.
  • Principle: Learn what must become true.
  • Options: Try one useful choice.
  • Resistance: Work with a partner at a safe pace.
  • Adaptation: Change the task when the first answer fails.

Teach the idea. Coach the person.

Class Timeline.

03 min

Arrival and Partner Match.

Students enter the studio and settle in. Sandy matches each student with a safe partner by age, size, and experience.

05 min

Useful Movement.

Students practice 2 to 3 basic movements. These include bridging, stepping over, and turning with balance.

06 min

Chest-to-Chest Connection.

The bottom student works to regain guard or stand up. The top student works to hold balance and control.

06 min

Chest-to-Back Control.

The defending student escapes to safety. The top student practices gentle control from behind.

06 min

Neutral Guard Game.

The top student passes the guard. The bottom student keeps distance, sweeps, or stands.

15 min

Resistance Rounds.

Short positional games connect each skill. Sandy keeps resistance calm and controlled.

04 min

Review and Room Reset.

Students review key escape moves. Everyone thanks their partners and resets the mats.

Your Partner Makes the Lesson Real.

A good training partner makes each lesson real. Both students have a clear job. Honest resistance helps you learn real skill. Partners care for one another. You test decisions safely, reset when needed, and make steady progress.

Beginner class & Rules.

A first-day student starts with one clear task, one useful movement, and guided practice.

The Beginner class gives them:

  • One selected partner.
  • One safety rule.
  • One useful movement.
  • One scoring target.

A beginner first learns how to tap, release, protect the neck, bridge, and move with controlled resistance.

Students train as part of the group from day one. Sandy keeps choices simple so every student feels confident.

Brief Demonstrations. More Guided Practice.

Each explanation follows the same sequence:

  1. See the starting position.
  2. Hear each student’s job.
  3. Watch one useful action.
  4. Learn how to score.
  5. Begin the round.

Most coach demonstrations stay under 45 seconds.

Sandy gives tips while students drill. He pauses the whole group only if several pairs need the same tip.

More Skill Means More Choices.

New students receive simple tasks and steady help. As you recognize positions, Sandy gives you new options.

The goal is not to copy every move. The goal is to see the puzzle, pick a useful move, and adapt as your partner moves. Sandy guides safety, partner matching, and safe resistance. You build confidence in your own choices.

What Parents Can Expect.

Parents are welcome to watch youth classes. During class, Sandy directs partner matches, practice pace, and room safety.

After class, Sandy shares quick progress notes. He highlights effort, listening, and good partner care. You can follow these milestones in the Progress Report Card.

Our goal is simple. We give kids daily chances to move, think, cooperate, and grow under positive coaching.

Why We Teach This Way.

Students learn best through direct practice. We keep problems small so you can begin. We add safe resistance so moves feel real. As your balance grows, you gain more freedom to explore.

What Students Review.

Class ends with one practical question:

  • Which movement helped you escape?
  • How did you hold the position?
  • Which space did you need to protect?
  • What will you try during the next class?

Students thank their partners, collect their gear, and help check the mat area.

New skills carry into the next class. Sandy keeps each class focused on what helps you most that day.

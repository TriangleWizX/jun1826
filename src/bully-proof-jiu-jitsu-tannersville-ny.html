Core Curriculum

Three Practical Skills to Practice

Coaches guide partner practice and calibrate the pace so students learn step by step.

1

Use Clear Words

Practice calm posture and speak with a clear voice. Setting a verbal boundary early often de-escalates peer conflict before it begins.

2

Make Space & Frame

Learn mechanical bone frames that keep you safe without striking. Create distance, protect balance, and move to a safe location.

3

Enlist a Trusted Adult

Tell a parent, coach, or school educator immediately. Martial arts training provides practice, but adults solve conflict together.

Parent FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Straightforward answers for parents considering martial arts for conflict de-escalation.

Is this a striking or punching class?
No. Sensei Sandy BJJ teaches Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, which focuses entirely on grappling, mechanical frames, balance control, and positional escapes. We do not teach striking, kicking, or aggressive fighting.
What should my child do if they encounter peer conflict?
We teach a 3-step escalation process: first, use clear words and body language to set a firm boundary. Second, make physical space and move toward a safe location. Third, report what happened to a teacher, parent, or trusted adult immediately.
Does Jiu-Jitsu replace guidance from school staff?
No. Martial arts training provides physical practice and calm self-assurance, but peer conflict resolution requires collaboration with teachers, school principals, and parents. We encourage open communication with school counselors.
Can parents watch class?
Yes. Parents are always welcome and encouraged to watch every class from our lobby seating area. You can observe how your child interacts with Sandy and their training partners.

Clear Choices

When peer conflict happens, children need a plan.

Parents want their children to stand tall, speak clearly, move with balance, and ask for help.

Bully-Proof Jiu-Jitsu gives young people practical choices. Students practice holding personal space, using firm words, and staying calm under pressure.

Coaches pair students by size and experience. Every drill moves at a cooperative, step-by-step pace.

The Sensei Sandy Method

Three core skills we build in class

Students learn practical tools for everyday school life before physical contact ever happens.

01

Voice

Students practice direct words: “Step back, please. I need space.” They practice speaking with calm posture and steady eye contact.

02

Space

Students practice head-up posture, active footing, and distance awareness. They learn to step back and walk toward school staff.

03

Control

If someone grabs their shirt or wrist, students use leverage, balance, and cooperative grips to break free and move to safety.

New York State Guidelines

For New York families: document, report, follow up

In New York, public schools follow the Dignity for All Students Act (DASA). Every school district assigns a Dignity Act Coordinator to support students.

Here is the recommended path for families:

  1. Document details: Write down what happened, note times and witnesses, and save text messages or social posts.
  2. Contact the school: Ask for the school Dignity Act Coordinator and request a copy of the reporting policy.
  3. Follow up: Keep communication in writing and schedule check-ins with teachers and staff.

Jiu-Jitsu training gives your child steady physical balance and clear verbal habits. Adults remain responsible for school rules, investigation, and discipline.

Common Questions

Bully-Proof Jiu-Jitsu FAQ

Is this a striking or fighting class?

This is pure Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu. Students practice body balance, safe movement, and calm positional control through pure grappling.

How does this prepare my child for tough situations?

The goal is to give your child clear boundaries, safe movement practice, and the calm physical confidence to defuse conflict early.

What should a child do first if peer conflict happens?

Step back to create space, speak with a calm voice, and tell a trusted teacher or parent right away.

What if someone grabs their wrist or jacket?

Students learn safe leverage, grip releases, and balance skills to break away cleanly and move to safety.

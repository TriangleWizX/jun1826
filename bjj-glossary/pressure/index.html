Pressure

Pressure is the use of body weight, alignment, timing, and connection to make a partner carry more load, lose comfort, and have less room to move.

Foundational concept Basics Common Chest to Chest Pin Side Control

Quick definition

Pressure is the use of body weight, alignment, timing, and connection to make a partner carry more load, lose comfort, and have less room to move.

Beginner translation

Pressure is more than being heavy. It is being connected in a way that makes your partner feel stuck and tired.

Why it matters

Pressure is the use of body weight, alignment, timing, and connection to make a partner carry more load, lose comfort, and have less room to move.

What beginners should know

A common mistake with pressure is using it passively instead of with structure and timing.

Common class phrases

  • You see pressure in chest-to-chest control
  • pinning exchanges
  • side control
  • and guard passing

Beginner Safety Cue

Safety cue: Move with control and communicate early while training pressure.

Related terms

Chest to Chest

Chest to chest is a close-control orientation where the top player keeps direct torso connection to limit movement and improve pressure.

Read more →

Pin

A pin is a form of control that keeps your partner from moving freely by anchoring part of their body to the mat or limiting their structure.

Read more →

Side Control

Side control is a top position where one person controls from across the other person’s upper body.

Read more →

Crossface

A crossface is a control where the top player uses the shoulder or arm across the head line to turn and flatten the bottom player.

Read more →

Guard Pass

A guard pass is the act of getting around, through, or past the bottom player's legs so the top player can reach stronger control like side control, north south, or mount.

FAQ

Is pressure only about bodyweight?

No. Alignment, timing, and connection matter as much as raw weight.

Can too much force disrupt pressure?

Yes. You control your pace as coaching gradually adds challenge burns energy and often creates openings for escapes.

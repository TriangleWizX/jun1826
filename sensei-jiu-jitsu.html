Understanding BJJ Lineage

How to Read a Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu Lineage

A martial arts lineage maps the transmission of knowledge from teacher to student across generations. In Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu (BJJ), tracing this lineage serves as a system of quality control, allowing academies and students to verify that their coach's credentials are based on a legitimate line of training and promotions starting from the foundations of the art.

However, it is important to distinguish between **historical roots** and a direct **instructional lineage**. While a historical root represents the legacy of influence and training relationships, an instructional lineage documents the exact chain of black-belt promotions. Historical influence and formal belt promotions do not always mean the same thing. For example, Carlos Gracie Jr. was the son of Carlos Gracie Sr. and trained within the wider Gracie family, including under Rolls Gracie. Similarly, Roberto Maia trained under Carlos Gracie Jr. at Gracie Barra in Rio de Janeiro, but formally received his black belt from Renzo Gracie in 1994.

At Sensei Sandy BJJ, we value transparency. Rather than presenting a single simplified chain that glosses over these training relationships, we split our lineage documentation into two sections: the broad historical roots of the art, and the direct, recent chain of active mentors who awarded the black belts leading directly to Sensei Sandy.

Part I

Historical Roots of BJJ

Tracing the transition from classical Japanese Jujutsu and Kodokan Judo to the development of early Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu.

Kanō Jigorō

Founder of Kodokan Judo

Kanō Jigorō (1860–1938) was a Japanese educator, athlete, and the founder of Kodokan Judo. Born in Mikage (now part of Kobe), Kanō studied classical Jujutsu styles, including Tenjin Shin'yō-ryū and Kitō-ryū, during a period when these martial arts were declining in Japan. In 1882, he founded the Kodokan, transforming combat techniques into a comprehensive educational system called Judo. Kanō's philosophy emphasized "Maximum Efficiency, Minimum Effort" (Seiryoku Zen'yo) and "Mutual Welfare and Benefit" (Jita Kyoei). He became the first Asian member of the International Olympic Committee in 1909 and worked tirelessly to integrate martial arts into Japanese public education and introduce Judo to the world.

Connection to this lineage: Kanō Jigorō is the root source of this lineage. He personally trained Mitsuyo Maeda at the Kodokan, passing on the principles of leverage, off-balancing (kuzushi), and live training (randori) that would travel to Brazil and form the core mechanics of Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu.

Mitsuyo Maeda

Judo Expert & Combat Pioneer

Mitsuyo Maeda (1878–1941) was a legendary Japanese judoka and professional prize fighter. Known in Brazil as "Conde Koma" (Count Combat), Maeda was one of the Kodokan's elite ambassadors sent by Kanō Jigorō to introduce Judo to the West. Maeda traveled through the United States, Central America, Europe, and South America, engaging in hundreds of public challenge matches against wrestlers, boxers, and savate fighters to prove the effectiveness of Japanese grappling. In 1914, he arrived in Belém, Brazil, where he eventually settled. Maeda's vast practical experience in real-world combat refined his grappling techniques, placing heavy emphasis on ground control and submissions, which he began teaching in Brazil.

Connection to this lineage: Maeda trained directly under Kanō Jigorō at the Kodokan in Tokyo. Upon settling in Belém, Brazil, Maeda met Carlos Gracie Sr. and began teaching him the martial art, establishing the historical bridge between Japanese Kodokan Judo and Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu.
Preceded by: Kanō Jigorō Followed by: Carlos Gracie Sr.

Carlos Gracie Sr.

Founder of Gracie Jiu-Jitsu

Carlos Gracie Sr. (1902–1994) was the founder of Gracie Jiu-Jitsu and the patriarch of the Gracie family. As a teenager in Belém, Carlos witnessed a public demonstration by Mitsuyo Maeda and became his student. Carlos absorbed Maeda's techniques and later moved to Rio de Janeiro, where he opened the first Gracie Jiu-Jitsu Academy in 1925. Along with his younger brother Hélio, Carlos adapted and refined the martial art to emphasize leverage over strength. Carlos was also a nutritionist, philosopher, businessman, developing the "Gracie Diet" and establishing the challenge match tradition ("Gracie Challenge") that popularized their style across Brazil.

Connection to this lineage: Carlos Gracie Sr. was Mitsuyo Maeda's first Brazilian student. He passed his knowledge to his brothers, including Hélio Gracie, and co-founded the Gracie Academy, laying the groundwork for the modern BJJ ranking system and family lineage.
Preceded by: Mitsuyo Maeda Followed by: Hélio Gracie
Carlos Gracie Biography Wikipedia Profile (Carlos) Source checked: July 2026

Hélio Gracie

Co-Founder of Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu

Hélio Gracie (1913–2009) was a co-founder of Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu and one of the most influential martial artists of the 20th century. Due to his frail physical health as a youth, Hélio could not participate in the intense physical training at his brother Carlos's academy, instead learning by watching. Recognizing that many traditional Japanese techniques relied on physical strength, Hélio modified them to focus on leverage, timing, and defensive guards. He tested these refinements in legendary, long-running challenge matches, most famously against judo champion Masahiko Kimura. His modifications allowed a smaller, weaker practitioner to defend against and defeat a larger, stronger attacker.

Connection to this lineage: Hélio Gracie trained under Carlos Gracie Sr. and collaborated with him to formalize Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu. He was a primary instructor to the next generation of Gracies, including Carlos Gracie Jr., shaping the technical foundations of the art.
Preceded by: Carlos Gracie Sr. Followed by: Carlos Gracie Jr.

Part II

Sensei Sandy’s Instructor Lineage

Exploring the direct instructor-student belt promotions that connect Sandy Jose Nunez to the Gracie Barra and Boston BJJ traditions.

Carlos Gracie Jr.

Gracie Barra Founder & IBJJF President

Carlos Gracie Jr. (born 1956) is a prominent Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu master, the founder of Gracie Barra, and the creator of the International Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu Federation (IBJJF). The son of Carlos Gracie Sr., Carlos Jr. trained extensively under both his father and his uncle Hélio Gracie, as well as under his legendary cousin Rolls Gracie. After Rolls's untimely passing in 1982, Carlos Jr. took over his instruction and later founded Gracie Barra in Barra da Tijuca, Rio de Janeiro. Under his leadership, Gracie Barra grew into one of the largest BJJ organizations globally, emphasizing community, structured curriculum, and competitive excellence, while the IBJJF standardized rules worldwide.

Connection to this lineage: Carlos Gracie Jr. represents the bridge between the founding Gracie generation and the global expansion of BJJ. He trained Roberto Maia at Gracie Barra in Rio de Janeiro before Maia moved to Boston to establish Boston BJJ.
Preceded by: Hélio Gracie Followed by: Roberto Maia
Carlos Gracie Jr. Profile Wikipedia Profile (Carlos Jr.) Source checked: July 2026

Roberto Maia

Founder of Boston Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu

Roberto Maia is a pioneer of Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu in New England and the founder of Boston Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, the first BJJ academy in Boston. Maia grew up in Rio de Janeiro, training under Carlos Gracie Jr. at the historic Gracie Barra academy alongside legendary figures like Jean Jacques Machado and Renzo Gracie. He was formally awarded his black belt by Renzo Gracie in 1994. Moving to Massachusetts, Maia introduced BJJ to a region dominated by traditional martial arts. Over three decades, Maia has coached thousands of students, including police officers, UFC fighters, and local families, earning a reputation for detailed, safety-first technical instruction and a welcoming training environment.

Connection to this lineage: Roberto Maia trained under Carlos Gracie Jr. at Gracie Barra and received his black belt from Renzo Gracie in 1994. He served as the primary instructor to Kenny Florian at Boston BJJ, bringing the Gracie tradition to the Northeast.
Preceded by: Carlos Gracie Jr. Followed by: Kenny Florian
Boston BJJ Academy Biography Source checked: July 2026

Kenny Florian

UFC Veteran & BJJ Black Belt

Kenneth Alan Florian (born 1976) is a retired American mixed martial artist, UFC veteran, and commentator. A former Division I soccer player at Boston College, Florian began training BJJ under Roberto Maia to stay active. He quickly excelled, earning his black belt from Maia and competing in prestigious tournaments like the Pan American Championships. Florian rose to national prominence on the inaugural season of *The Ultimate Fighter* in 2005, going on to challenge for UFC titles in multiple weight classes. Known for his elite technical grappling and articulate analysis, Florian later served as a UFC color commentator and currently works as a PFL commentator and co-host of the Anik & Florian Podcast.

Connection to this lineage: Kenny Florian earned his BJJ black belt from Roberto Maia at Boston BJJ. Florian was a close mentor, training partner, and instructor to Josh Griffiths, who trained with him in Boston and New York.
Preceded by: Roberto Maia Followed by: Josh Griffiths

Josh Griffiths

4th Degree Black Belt • Founder & Head Instructor, Clockwork Jiu-Jitsu

Joshua Griffiths (born April 25, 1980 in California) is a 4th degree BJJ black belt under former UFC contender Kenny Florian and the head instructor of Clockwork Jiu-Jitsu in NYC. His grappling journey began in 2001 after attending UFC 32 in New Jersey while studying engineering at Stevens Institute of Technology. Searching for training in NYC, he joined Fabio Clemente's Alliance academy at Studio-X, quickly becoming a top student and coach. Training extensively with Marcelo Garcia after Garcia arrived in 2006 boosted Josh's competitive career, leading to his 2007 Pan American Championship win as a brown belt. Following a high-profile 2007 academy transition and dismissed lawsuit at Studio-X, Josh received his BJJ black belt from Kenny Florian in December 2007 and established Clockwork Jiu-Jitsu into a premier Manhattan academy.

Competing in the Meio Pesado (88kg/194lbs) division, Josh won dozens of tournaments, including the 2007 Pan American Championship (brown), 2010 & 2011 World Pro Cup Trials, multiple Abu Dhabi World Pro trials, and Copa Atlantica titles (2004–2006 weight & absolute). Known for his signature Cross Choke, his coaching style is fun, technical, and honest. He specializes in teaching students how to feel heavy and properly distribute weight—a core skill based on leverage rather than athleticism.

Owner & Head Coach, Sensei Sandy BJJ

Sandy Jose Nunez is a Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu black belt, the head instructor of Sensei Sandy BJJ in Tannersville, NY, and the founder of Jiu Jitsu Sandy. Sandy began his Jiu-Jitsu journey in New York City, dedicating years of training under Josh Griffiths at Clockwork Jiu Jitsu. Sandy's coaching philosophy is centered on safety, structured progression, and a calm, supportive learning environment. He is dedicated to helping kids, teens, and adults build confidence, coordination, and functional skill at a comfortable pace.

Connection to this lineage: Sandy Jose Nunez trained from white belt to black belt under Josh Griffiths at Clockwork Jiu Jitsu in NYC. He is the head instructor at Sensei Sandy BJJ in Tannersville, NY, bringing this prestigious lineage directly to the Catskills.

Sandy Jose Nunez was awarded his Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu black belt by Josh Griffiths, the head instructor of Clockwork Jiu Jitsu in New York City. The promotion followed years of dedicated training under Josh's direct guidance.

Josh Griffiths trained in Boston, Massachusetts, under Roberto Maia (founder of Boston Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu) and alongside UFC veteran Kenny Florian. After earning his black belt, Josh moved to New York City to establish Clockwork Jiu Jitsu, which has grown into a premier NYC academy.

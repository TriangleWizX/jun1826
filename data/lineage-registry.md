# Lineage Source & Verification Registry

This registry maintains the metadata, validation status, and source material for the Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu lineage at Sensei Sandy BJJ. It is updated periodically to ensure all external links are active and all historical records remain accurate.

**Last Full Verification Audit:** July 2026

---

## 1. Registry Schema

Each member of the lineage is registered with the following fields:
- **Member ID:** Unique lowercase slug identifier.
- **Display Name:** Canonical spelling for UI display.
- **Name Variants:** Common alternative spellings or nicknames.
- **Role:** Title/rank relevance to BJJ or Judo.
- **Historical vs Direct:** Traced as *Historical Roots* (broad legacy of development) or *Instructor Lineage* (direct belt promotions).
- **Preceded By:** Immediate predecessor in the line.
- **Followed By:** Immediate successor in the line.
- **Profile Anchor:** Target ID on `/sensei-jiu-jitsu`.
- **Short Bio:** Summary of life and contributions (80-140 words).
- **Connection Copy:** Explicit context of their relationship in this lineage.
- **Primary Source:** Verified external resource URL.
- **Backup Source:** Supporting citation or Wikipedia link.
- **Source Checked Date:** Last time links were verified as working (HTTP 200).
- **Portrait Filename:** Relative path to the optimized WebP asset.
- **Portrait Credit:** Author or source of the portrait.
- **Portrait License:** Usage license under copyright law.

### 1. Kanō Jigorō
- **Member ID:** `jigoro-kano`
- **Display Name:** Kanō Jigorō
- **Name Variants:** Jigoro Kano, Shihan Kano
- **Role:** Founder of Kodokan Judo
- **Historical vs Direct:** Historical Roots
- **Preceded By:** None (Root Source)
- **Followed By:** Mitsuyo Maeda
- **Profile Anchor:** `#jigoro-kano`
- **Short Bio:** Kanō Jigorō (1860–1938) was a Japanese educator, athlete, and the founder of Kodokan Judo. Born in Mikage (now part of Kobe), Kanō studied classical Jujutsu styles, including Tenjin Shin'yō-ryū and Kitō-ryū, during a period when these martial arts were declining in Japan. In 1882, he founded the Kodokan, transforming combat techniques into a comprehensive educational system called Judo. Kanō's philosophy emphasized "Maximum Efficiency, Minimum Effort" (Seiryoku Zen'yo) and "Mutual Welfare and Benefit" (Jita Kyoei). He became the first Asian member of the International Olympic Committee in 1909 and worked tirelessly to integrate martial arts into Japanese public education and introduce Judo to the world.
- **Connection Copy:** Kanō Jigorō is the root source of this lineage. He personally trained Mitsuyo Maeda at the Kodokan, passing on the principles of leverage, off-balancing (kuzushi), and live training (randori) that would travel to Brazil and form the core mechanics of Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu.
- **Primary Source:** [IJF Hall of Fame Biography](https://www.ijf.org/history/hall-of-fame/96)

### 2. Mitsuyo Maeda
- **Member ID:** `mitsuyo-maeda`
- **Display Name:** Mitsuyo Maeda
- **Name Variants:** Conde Koma, Count Combat, Otávio Maeda
- **Role:** Judo Expert & Combat Pioneer
- **Historical vs Direct:** Historical Roots
- **Preceded By:** Kanō Jigorō
- **Followed By:** Carlos Gracie Sr.
- **Profile Anchor:** `#mitsuyo-maeda`
- **Short Bio:** Mitsuyo Maeda (1878–1941) was a legendary Japanese judoka and professional prize fighter. Known in Brazil as "Conde Koma" (Count Combat), Maeda was one of the Kodokan's elite ambassadors sent by Kanō Jigorō to introduce Judo to the West. Maeda traveled through the United States, Central America, Europe, and South America, engaging in hundreds of public challenge matches against wrestlers, boxers, and savate fighters to prove the effectiveness of Japanese grappling. In 1914, he arrived in Belém, Brazil, where he eventually settled. Maeda's vast practical experience in real-world combat refined his grappling techniques, placing heavy emphasis on ground control and submissions, which he began teaching in Brazil.
- **Connection Copy:** Maeda trained directly under Kanō Jigorō at the Kodokan in Tokyo. Upon settling in Belém, Brazil, Maeda met Carlos Gracie Sr. and began teaching him the martial art, establishing the historical bridge between Japanese Kodokan Judo and Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu.

### 3. Carlos Gracie Sr.
- **Member ID:** `carlos-gracie`

- **Name Variants:** Carlos Gracie
- **Role:** Founder of Gracie Jiu-Jitsu
- **Historical vs Direct:** Historical Roots
- **Preceded By:** Mitsuyo Maeda
- **Followed By:** Hélio Gracie
- **Profile Anchor:** `#carlos-gracie`
- **Short Bio:** Carlos Gracie Sr. (1902–1994) was the founder of Gracie Jiu-Jitsu and the patriarch of the Gracie family. As a teenager in Belém, Carlos witnessed a public demonstration by Mitsuyo Maeda and became his student. Carlos absorbed Maeda's techniques and later moved to Rio de Janeiro, where he opened the first Gracie Jiu-Jitsu Academy in 1925. Along with his younger brother Hélio, Carlos adapted and refined the martial art to emphasize leverage over strength. Carlos was also a nutritionist, philosopher, and businessman, developing the "Gracie Diet" and establishing the challenge match tradition ("Gracie Challenge") that popularized their style across Brazil.
- **Connection Copy:** Carlos Gracie Sr. was Mitsuyo Maeda's first Brazilian student. He passed his knowledge to his brothers, including Hélio Gracie, and co-founded the Gracie Academy, laying the groundwork for the modern BJJ ranking system and family lineage.

### 4. Hélio Gracie
- **Member ID:** `helio-gracie`
- **Display Name:** Hélio Gracie
- **Name Variants:** Helio Gracie
- **Role:** Co-Founder of Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu
- **Historical vs Direct:** Historical Roots
- **Preceded By:** Carlos Gracie Sr.
- **Followed By:** Carlos Gracie Jr.
- **Profile Anchor:** `#helio-gracie`
- **Short Bio:** Hélio Gracie (1913–2009) was a co-founder of Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu and one of the most influential martial artists of the 20th century. Due to his frail physical health as a youth, Hélio could not participate in the intense physical training at his brother Carlos's academy, instead learning by watching. Recognizing that many traditional Japanese techniques relied on physical strength, Hélio modified them to focus on leverage, timing, and defensive guards. He tested these refinements in legendary, long-running challenge matches, most famously against judo champion Masahiko Kimura. His modifications allowed a smaller, weaker practitioner to defend against and defeat a larger, stronger attacker.
- **Connection Copy:** Hélio Gracie trained under Carlos Gracie Sr. and collaborated with him to formalize Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu. He was a primary instructor to the next generation of Gracies, including Carlos Gracie Jr., shaping the technical foundations of the art.

- **Role:** Gracie Barra Founder & IBJJF President

- **Preceded By:** Hélio Gracie
- **Followed By:** Roberto Maia
- **Profile Anchor:** `#carlos-gracie-jr`
- **Short Bio:** Carlos Gracie Jr. (born 1956) is a prominent Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu master, the founder of Gracie Barra, and the creator of the International Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu Federation (IBJJF). The son of Carlos Gracie Sr., Carlos Jr. trained extensively under both his father and his uncle Hélio Gracie, as well as under his legendary cousin Rolls Gracie. After Rolls's untimely passing in 1982, Carlos Jr. took over his instruction and later founded Gracie Barra in Barra da Tijuca, Rio de Janeiro. Under his leadership, Gracie Barra grew into one of the largest BJJ organizations globally, emphasizing community, structured curriculum, and competitive excellence, while the IBJJF standardized rules worldwide.
- **Connection Copy:** Carlos Gracie Jr. represents the bridge between the founding Gracie generation and the global expansion of BJJ. He trained Roberto Maia at Gracie Barra in Rio de Janeiro before Maia moved to Boston to establish Boston BJJ.

### 6. Roberto Maia
- **Member ID:** `roberto-maia`
- **Display Name:** Roberto Maia
- **Name Variants:** Roberto Maia Brandao
- **Role:** Founder of Boston Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu
- **Historical vs Direct:** Instructor Lineage (Black Belt from Renzo Gracie)
- **Preceded By:** Carlos Gracie Jr.
- **Followed By:** Kenny Florian
- **Profile Anchor:** `#roberto-maia`
- **Short Bio:** Roberto Maia is a pioneer of Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu in New England and the founder of Boston Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, the first BJJ academy in Boston. Maia grew up in Rio de Janeiro, training under Carlos Gracie Jr. at the historic Gracie Barra academy alongside legendary figures like Jean Jacques Machado and Renzo Gracie. He was formally awarded his black belt by Renzo Gracie in 1994. Moving to Massachusetts, Maia introduced BJJ to a region dominated by traditional martial arts. Over three decades, Maia has coached thousands of students, including police officers, UFC fighters, and local families, earning a reputation for detailed, safety-first technical instruction and a welcoming training environment.
- **Connection Copy:** Roberto Maia trained under Carlos Gracie Jr. at Gracie Barra and received his black belt from Renzo Gracie in 1994. He served as the primary instructor to Kenny Florian at Boston BJJ, bringing the Gracie tradition to the Northeast.
